# client: world_map

The world map: the surface as a lit 3D terrain mesh rendered by the GPU. A 400×400 square window of terrain around the player with flat earth faces on its edges (the prototype's composition, see the Legend entry in `decisions.md`) with modern lighting. Left-click travels to the clicked point. Right-drag turns (sideways) and tilts (up/down) the view around the player, the wheel zooms, Home returns to the starting view. The window stays lined up with the camera and keeps its size on screen: turning turns the world inside it, zooming shows less of the world in it. Entities with a `common::Token` (including the player) are drawn as tokens.

## Files

- `crates/client/src/world_map/mod.rs`: `WorldMapPlugin`. `spawn_view` (Startup) spawns a `Camera3d` with an `OrbitCamera` (tonemapping and debanding off), an east sun with shadows, ambient light, the terrain and edge-face meshes, and inserts `HeightCache` and `TokenAssets`; `TerrainGrid` is inited by the plugin. `update_terrain` (runs when `MapPosition`, clock, weather, style or the camera's yaw or zoom changes) moves the terrain to the player's grid point, fills the grid and cuts the window and edge faces for `view_square(orbit)`; `token::resize_tokens` rebuilds the outline meshes in place when the window height or zoom changes; `token::sync_tokens` and `token::sync_icons` keep token views matching `Token`s and `Icon`s; `place_camera_and_tokens` (every frame) places the camera around the player's `SmoothPosition`, writes `common::ViewFacing`, puts each token view at its owner's `SmoothPosition` and turns/places icon anchors. In `PlayerSystems::Input`: `camera::control_with_mouse`, then `pick_on_click` (sends `TravelTo`). Constants `HEIGHT_SCALE`, `HALF_SIZE` (200), `CAMERA_FOV_DEGREES` (20), `CAMERA_DISTANCE` and `CAMERA_PITCH_DEGREES` (the starting view), `LOOK_AHEAD`, `SLOPE_SHADING`, `SUN_LUX`, `AMBIENT_BRIGHTNESS`. `view_square` (the window for the camera: its ground axes, half-size `HALF_SIZE × zoom`).
- `crates/client/src/world_map/camera.rs`: `OrbitCamera` (component: pub `yaw`, `pitch`, `distance`; `new(pitch, distance, look_ahead)`, `reset()`, `transform(target)`, `ground_axes()` (right and towards-camera along the ground), `quarter_turns()`, `zoom()` (distance / starting distance), `home_pitch()`), `control_with_mouse` (right-drag turns and tilts every `OrbitCamera`, `RADIANS_PER_PIXEL` 0.005; pitch 10°–85°; wheel zooms ×0.9 per notch, between the starting distance (the furthest out) and `MIN_ZOOM` 0.6 of it, both the owner's choice; Home resets all three). Nothing world-map specific, so other 3D views can reuse it. Unit tests.
- `crates/client/src/world_map/picking.rs`: `pick(ray, centre, square, ...) -> Option<(x, y)>`: walks the camera ray in 0.5-unit steps until it is below the drawn surface; misses if it passes over or comes in through an edge face. Inside/outside uses the current `ViewSquare`. Unit tests.
- `crates/client/src/world_map/token.rs`: how `Token`s are drawn. `TokenAssets` (resource: one body and one outline mesh per shape, the icon quad, one lit body material per attitude and one outline material, each in a normal and a completed version; `icon_offset(camera_rotation, pitch, home_pitch)`), `prism` (the shape extruded upwards: `Cylinder` for the circle, `Extrusion<RegularPolygon>` stood up for the others; `DIAMETER` 16, thickness 15%), `TokenView` (separate root entity per token: body, outline and `IconAnchor` children), `sync_tokens` (spawns views for new tokens, swaps mesh/material when a `Token` changes, despawns views whose owner or token is gone), `sync_icons` (puts the owner's `Icon` on the view's `IconAnchor` as an unlit, alpha-masked quad with nearest-pixel sampling; one material per icon file, cached in `TokenAssets`). Outline: the same prism grown by `OUTLINE_PIXELS` (2, halved by the owner) in grid units (from the camera's current distance, FOV and window height; `outline_width`), `cull_mode: Front` (inverted hull, no custom shader); polygon corners grow by `outline / cos(π/sides)` so the rim is even.
- `crates/client/src/world_map/terrain.rs`: `HeightCache` (wrapping 640×640 grid of surface samples). `ViewSquare` (the window on show: `right`, `towards`, `half`; `contains`). `TerrainGrid` (resource: wrapping 640×640 store of per-point drawn height, ground height, normal and colour with baked slope shading, keyed by world position; `fill` works out only points the window's bounding box needs that aren't kept yet, `redraw_all` clears it). `build_window` (the terrain mesh: whole triangles inside the turned square are copied, ones crossing its border are clipped by `clip` (Sutherland–Hodgman), so the outline is exact). `build_edges` (one unlit mesh with faces down all four window edges: water depth strip over earth down to `FRONT_BASE` × water level in the starting view, the depth below the water scaled by the zoom so the frame keeps its shape; columns at every grid line and triangle diagonal the edge crosses (`crossings`), heights from the triangles (`height_at`), so the face top meets the terrain exactly; wound to face outwards, so back-face culling hides the far ones). `highest_shown_height` (max drawn height within a radius, for resting tokens). Unit tests.

## Public API

- `WorldMapPlugin`.
- `OrbitCamera` (component on the map camera): read or set `yaw` (radians; 0 looks north, positive moves the camera east), `pitch` (radians above the horizon), `distance` (the window shrinks with it). Used by `dev_tools` (`GAME_VIEW`).

## Depends on

- `common` (`CurrentWorld`, `GameClock`, `ViewFacing`, `ViewStats`, `Weather`, `Token`, `Icon`), `player` (`MapPosition`, `SmoothPosition`, `TravelTo`, `PlayerSystems`), `surface_style`.

## Used by

- `dev_tools` (`OrbitCamera`).

## Notes

- World `x` → 3D `+X`, world `y` (south) → `+Z`. The terrain and edge faces sit at the player's grid point (`MapPosition`) and move a whole step at a time; the camera and tokens follow `SmoothPosition`, so travel looks smooth (the window edges shift by under one unit relative to the camera).
- Turning and zooming (owner's choice): the window is a square lined up with the camera, cut out of the world-aligned terrain, rather than the slab itself turning on screen. Zooming moves the camera in and shrinks the square by the same factor, so the square keeps its size on screen and shows less of the world (a true zoom: hills look taller). Each turn or zoom step re-cuts the window. Grid triangles are split along the north-east/south-west diagonal; the edge faces and `TerrainGrid::height_at` follow that same split.
- Camera: narrow field of view (20°) from far away (a "long lens"), so perspective is flat and the window's sides stay near vertical. It looks at **sea level** (not the ground), ahead of the player towards the camera by `LOOK_AHEAD` in the starting view (shrinking as it zooms in or looks down), so it doesn't bob over hills and the near face fits on screen. The view stays where it is left. If the FOV changes, scale `CAMERA_DISTANCE` by `tan(old/2) / tan(new/2)` to keep the framing; `far` is twice the starting distance (the furthest zoom).
- `ViewFacing` (common) is the camera's yaw rounded to a quarter turn; `player` uses it so arrow keys step towards the top of the screen. Written with `set_if_neq`.
- Shading: real lighting alone looked too smooth, so the prototype's slope shading (height minus the western neighbour, very strong) is baked into the vertex colours, scaled by `SLOPE_SHADING`. On top, a harsh sun (from the east) with little ambient fill gives shadows and dark far sides. Both are fixed to the world, so they stay put when the view turns (looking south, the map is lit from the left and looks darker). Brightness was tuned for the starting view.
- Water is part of the terrain mesh, not a separate plane: points below the water are drawn at the surface (`SurfaceStyle::shown_height`) and coloured by depth, as the prototype did. This keeps shore sand (just below the water by definition) visible at the waterline. A translucent plane showed it tinted green, and lifting only the sand made spikes.
- Colours: `Tonemapping::None` on the camera; Bevy's default tonemapping made the palette pastel. With it off, sunlit faces can exceed full brightness and clip towards lime, so keep `SUN_LUX` low.
- Brightness was tuned to roughly match the prototype's renderer (mean luminance ~77, measured from `dev_tools` screenshots with Pillow). If colours or lighting change, re-measure.
- Frozen water vertex colours are multiplied by `ICE_BOOST` (1.41, above white) to cancel the sun's dimming of flat surfaces, so it shows at `#eee`. Re-measure if the lighting changes.
- Edge face bottom is `FRONT_BASE` (0.3) × the water level in the starting view rather than 0 (shorter than the prototype's), closer to the water when zoomed in; where the ground is below that, the water strip stops at the base.
- Tokens rest on the highest drawn ground within their radius, so they never sink into slopes (they float a little over the low side). The outline stays 2 screen pixels wide across window resizes and zoom (`resize_tokens`); icons scale with the map instead.
- Token placement reads the owner's `player::SmoothPosition`. Only the player has a token so far; when other entities get one, their position component should move to `common` (or `shared`), and tokens outside the window will need hiding.
- Token views never rotate (shape turns are baked into the meshes), so an `IconAnchor`'s rotation copied from the camera makes it face the screen. The icon is a quad child of the anchor, so it always shows the same face whatever the camera does. Icons are sized relative to the token, not the screen (owner's choice, for a blockier retro look): the central 32×32 picture is `ICON_ART_SIZE` (1.5 × the token's diameter) wide, so art pixels aren't whole screen pixels. The anchor sits on the token's top, moved up the screen (along the camera's up, so it works at any tilt). In the starting view the feet (the bottom of the central picture, less `ICON_SHADOW_PIXELS`: 3 art pixels, as most pictures end in a shadow that far up) are `ICON_GAP_PIXELS` (1 art pixel) above the top's centre, so characters stand on it. As the camera tilts down they slide (by the pitch's sine) to `FEET_ABOVE_EDGE_PIXELS` (2 screen pixels) above the token's near edge, outline included (owner's choice), and the anchor moves towards the camera (up to half the token's diameter, invisible on screen) so the nearly flat icon doesn't dip into the token's top. Completed tokens don't dim their icon (yet). The current art (Gervais tiles) is planned to be replaced by commissioned artwork: retune `ICON_FILE_PIXELS`, `ICON_ART_PIXELS`, `ICON_ART_SIZE`, `ICON_GAP_PIXELS` and `ICON_SHADOW_PIXELS` to match it. Polygons have a corner pointing north, then turn: the square to line up with the grid, the hexagon (30°) and octagon (22.5°) to put a flat side towards the camera in the starting view (turning the view turns that side away).
- Completed tokens (`fade`): sRGB colour × `COMPLETED_SHADE` (0.7) and alpha `COMPLETED_OPACITY` (0.9, chosen by the owner) with `AlphaMode::AlphaToCoverage` (needs MSAA, Bevy's default), for body and outline alike. Ordinary blending showed the outline's inner faces (directly behind the body) through the token, turning it black; alpha to coverage still writes depth, so the body hides them. Coverage comes in coarse steps (measured: 0.85 and 0.9 look identical), so small opacity changes may do nothing, and it looks slightly dotted up close.
- Performance (native dev build, shown in the HUD): a step fills ~1 ms (only the new edge) and cuts the window in ~9–12 ms in the starting view (more at diagonal angles, less zoomed in: ~4 ms at 0.6); turning costs the same cut every frame while dragging. A full refill (new hour, weather or style, e.g. every frame of the temperature demo) is ~15 ms facing north and ~28 ms at 45°, plus the cut. The very first fill is ~100 ms (sampling everything). Measured before this change: ~12 ms per step, full update in place.
- Terrain and edge faces use `NoFrustumCulling` because their meshes are replaced after the bounds are computed. `HeightCache` is inserted by commands at startup, so systems take it as `Option` for the first frame.
