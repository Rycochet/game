# client: world_map

The world map: the surface as a lit 3D terrain mesh rendered by the GPU, with the pois this character knows of (caves, houses, castles...) as small 3D models. A 400×400 square window of terrain around the player, drawn from square chunks of the grid and cut to the window by the terrain's shaders, with flat earth faces on its edges (the prototype's composition, see the Legend entry in `decisions.md`) with modern lighting. Left-click travels to the clicked point; clicking a poi's token travels to it and enters its site (`site`). The camera (right-drag turns and tilts, wheel zooms, Home resets) and the tokens come from `view3d`. The window stays lined up with the camera and keeps its size on screen: turning turns the world inside it, zooming shows less of the world in it. Shown while `GameView::WorldMap` is current.

## Files

- `crates/client/src/world_map/mod.rs`: `WorldMapPlugin`. `spawn_view` (Startup) spawns the lights, the `Terrain` entity (the chunks' parent) and the edge-face meshes (all marked `WorldMapScene`, hidden unless this is the current view) and inserts `HeightCache`; `TerrainGrid` is inited by the plugin. `show_scene` / `hide_scene` on entering/leaving the view (also sets the ambient light and `view3d::ViewGrid`: 1 unit per point, floor at sea level). Only while current: `update_terrain` (runs when `MapPosition`, clock, weather, style or the camera's yaw or zoom changes; changes made while another view was current are still noticed) fills the grid points of every chunk the window touches (`terrain::chunks_needed`) and cuts the edge faces, rivers and roads for `view_square(orbit)`; `chunks::update_chunks` (chained after it): `TerrainChunks`, the chunks as children of `Terrain`, at their world position with the terrain material, built once, again when their grid points are worked out again (`TerrainGrid::take_changed`), dropped when out of reach; adds its count and time to the HUD line; `rest_tokens_on_terrain` (after `View3dSystems::Place`) rests every token view (pois, gates, players, mobs) with its centre on the drawn ground at its own point; `hide_tokens_outside_window` (same time) hides token views not wholly inside the window (centre within `half` less the token radius) and `hide_scene` shows them all again; `pick_on_click` (in `PlayerSystems::Input` after `View3dSystems::Click`, sends `TravelTo` unless a token was clicked). `light_from`: which way along the ground the brightest light is at an hour, for slope shading. Rivers' strips are still clipped by `clip` (Sutherland–Hodgman). `build_edges` (one unlit mesh with faces down all four window edges: water depth strip over earth down to `FRONT_BASE` × water level in the starting view, the depth below the water scaled by the zoom so the frame keeps its shape; columns at every grid line and triangle diagonal the edge crosses (`crossings`), heights from the triangles (`height_at`), so the face top meets the terrain exactly; wound to face outwards, so back-face culling hides the far ones). `shown_height_at` (the drawn surface's height at a fractional point, woods included, on the same triangles as `TerrainGrid::height_at` but straight from the cache, for resting tokens). Unit tests. The clearing is in `rivers/clearing.rs`, round roads too (`ROAD_CLEARED` from their edge, so wider roads clear wider), redone whenever `KnownRoads::changes` moves.
- `crates/client/src/world_map/roads.rs`: what drawing roads shares: `width(kind)` (1 to 4 px at the starting zoom, like rivers), `smoothed(road)` (corners averaged out, relative to its first point), `east_of` (the nearer way round the planet). `terrain::build_roads` makes the one mesh (`RoadStrips`, the rivers' material, alpha 1 so not water) from `CurrentWorld::roads`: paths the land's own colour lightened (`PATH_WORN`, keeping the hue), tracks brown, stone roads grey, highways pale with dark edges side by side (overlapping strips flickered); larger kinds a little higher (`LIFT_PER_KIND`), so the larger is on top where they overlap (owner), all below rivers. Under snow, paths vanish and the rest show as darker snow (`SHOWS_THROUGH_SNOW`). Within a point of a river they are drawn as a bridge above it, planks for paths and tracks, stone for the rest.
- `crates/client/src/world_map/sky.rs`: `spawn`, `update` (every frame: the time and window for the shaders; once a second, or at once after a teleport, samples `shared::weather` onto a 100×100 texture with 10-point spacing, about 1.8 ms: red cover, green rain, blue temperature. Two such textures, a second apart, are faded between, so the sky changes smoothly), `TerrainMaterial` (`ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, TerrainSky>`), `CloudMaterial`, `PrecipitationMaterial`, `SkyAssets`. `SkyParams::light` (`SkyNow::lit`) dims clouds and precipitation with the sky; its alpha is the glow round the player (`PLAYER_GLOW`, more the darker it is). In `sky/`: `materials.rs`, `spawn.rs`, `update.rs`.
- Video settings (`Preferences::video`): `lights::update` sets the shadow map size (Low 1024, High 2048), casts no shadows with Shadows off, and raises the ambient fill and edge faces to Brightness; `SkyParams::shown` (clouds, cloud shadows only with Shadows on too, the share of rain, foliage) and `still` (Brightness, and the seconds water moves by, 0 with Reduce Motion) go to the shaders; `view_square(orbit, distance)` and `drawn_half` give the window for View Distance (Near ¾, Far the whole `MAX_HALF` even zoomed in; the minimap keeps `window_half`); `rest_tokens_on_terrain` goes by where tokens are drawn, so snapped ones rest right too.
- `crates/client/assets/shaders/`: `terrain.wesl` (the window cut, then standard lighting plus cloud shadows, water ripples and painted vegetation), `terrain_prepass.wesl` (the same cut in the shadow pass; the terrain's base material is `AlphaMode::Mask(0.0)`, which keeps every pixel but makes Bevy use it), `clouds.wesl` (the sheet), `precipitation.wesl`. `struct Sky` in each must match `SkyParams` in `sky.rs`.

## Notes

- Sky (owner's design): clouds are one sheet at `CLOUD_HEIGHT` (170, may change), straight above the window and exactly its shape, drawn in chunky blocks (`BLOCK` in `clouds.wesl`, about 8 screen pixels), so they end in a hard edge and don't move relative to the map when the camera turns. Being high and nearer the camera, the sheet shows further up the screen and wider than the map. Clicks pass through. Under them the terrain is slightly darkened straight below (`SHADOW`), not along the sun's direction, and not real shadows. Water is marked by vertex alpha 0 (frozen water isn't), and flowing rivers count as water.
- Vegetation is painted, not modelled: `shared::vegetation` per grid point reaches the fragment shader in the terrain mesh's UV slots (`ATTRIBUTE_UV_0` trees and low growth, zero except on `SurfaceKind::Land`; `ATTRIBUTE_UV_1` how narrow and how lush), so clipping and interpolation carry it with no vertex shader of our own. `terrain.wesl` draws crowns on a jittered grid (`CROWN`, conical where narrow, thin woods losing crowns one at a time) and grass, changing the existing colour, so biome blends and hue rotation carry through, and tilting `pbr_input.N` for the bumps. Detail fades by `fwidth` before it reaches pixel size. Woods are also really raised: `terrain::canopy_lift` adds to each point's drawn height (`CANOPY_HEIGHT` 2.5, plus `CANOPY_LUSH` 1.5 in rainforest), fading to nothing towards the shore (`CANOPY_SHORE`, or the shore stretches up a wall) and round rivers (`KnownRivers::open_at`, which also thins the trees), and counting only a little in the baked slope shading (`CANOPY_SHADING`, or woods' edges go white). Normals, edge faces, rivers, token resting and click picking all follow the raised surface; `ground` stays the real ground, which is also how edge faces know land from water. Rivers sit in a fold, so low camera angles can hide stretches of them. Rivers use the same material without UVs (`@if(VERTEX_UVS_A && VERTEX_UVS_B)`). Woods are darker than open land, which lowers the map's mean brightness.
- Precipitation is `DROPS` quads placed by the vertex shader from random numbers in their positions, each at a fixed place on the map (a pattern repeating every `DROP_TILE`, so walking doesn't move them): how many show follows rain; temperature picks snow (below 0.2), sleet (below 0.3), otherwise rain, or hail where rain is above 0.8. Time comes from `common::ServerClock`, so clients agree to within their latency.

- World `x` → 3D `+X`, world `y` (south) → `+Z`. The terrain and edge faces sit at the player's grid point (`MapPosition`) and move a whole step at a time; the camera and tokens follow `SmoothPosition`, so travel looks smooth (the window edges shift by under one unit relative to the camera).
- Turning and zooming (owner's choice): the window is a square lined up with the camera, cut out of the world-aligned terrain, rather than the slab itself turning on screen. Zooming moves the camera in and shrinks the square by the same factor, so the square keeps its size on screen and shows less of the world (a true zoom: hills look taller). The terrain's chunks stay as they are; the shaders cut the new square, and only the edge faces, rivers and roads are cut again. Grid triangles are split along the north-east/south-west diagonal; the edge faces and `TerrainGrid::height_at` follow that same split.
- Shading: real lighting alone looked too smooth, so the prototype's slope shading (height minus the height a step away from the light, very strong) is baked into the vertex colours, scaled by `SLOPE_SHADING`, and follows the brightest light once a game hour, when the terrain is redrawn anyway (owner's choice: once a real minute is fine). On top, the suns and moons (`shared::celestial`) with little ambient fill give real shadows, including mountains' at dawn and dusk, and dark far sides; hills outside the window cast none. Both are fixed to the world, so they stay put when the view turns. Brightness was tuned for the starting view.
- Water is part of the terrain mesh, not a separate plane: points below the water are drawn at the surface (`SurfaceStyle::shown_height`) and coloured by depth, as the prototype did. This keeps the shore (just below the water by definition) visible at the waterline. A translucent plane showed it tinted green, and lifting only the shore made spikes.
- Lightning (`common::Lightning`, owner, 2026-11-08): everything flashes. `lights::update` adds `FLASH_AMBIENT` to the ambient fill, tinted `FLASH_COLOUR`, and lifts the edge faces towards `FLASH_LIT`; `sky::update` lifts `SkyParams::light` the same way, so clouds and rain light up. Tuned by eye in screenshots at night: the ground goes pale and poi models nearly white. Bolts (`bolts.rs`, `strike`: each `common::BoltStruck` inside the window, the nearer way round the planet) stand on the drawn ground (`shown_height_at`) and reach the clouds (`sky::CLOUD_HEIGHT`, at least `LEAST_HEIGHT`), `BOLT_WIDTH` 1.5 units, drawn by `lightning::spawn_bolt` and marked `WorldMapScene`.
- Glow round the player (owner's idea): `terrain.wesl` adds some of the ground's own colour after lighting, so shadows don't matter: full out to `GLOW_FULL` (one chunk), gone by `GLOW_END`, and none in full light, where it would clip. Only ground and rivers get it, not poi models or bases. Each client draws it for its own player.
- Colours: `Tonemapping::None` on the camera; Bevy's default tonemapping made the palette pastel. With it off, sunlit faces can exceed full brightness and clip towards lime, so keep `FLAT_LUX` low; `MOST_FLAT_LIGHT` holds down several suns together.
- Brightness was tuned to roughly match the prototype's renderer (mean luminance ~77, measured from `dev_tools` screenshots with Pillow). If colours or lighting change, re-measure.
- Frozen water vertex colours are multiplied by `ICE_BOOST` (1.41, above white) to cancel the sun's dimming of flat surfaces, so it shows at `#eee`. Re-measure if the lighting changes.
- Edge face bottom is `FRAME_DEPTH` (25) below the lowest drawn point in the window (`TerrainGrid::lowest_shown`) in the starting view, less when zoomed in: measured from the ground in view rather than the sea, so highlands on worlds in metres stand on a frame as deep as lowlands. Picking looks up to `MAX_HEIGHT` 256, as surfaces in metres pass 128.
- Largest window (owner's rule: debug values must render correctly too): `MAX_HALF` (= `HALF_SIZE`, so only zooming out further than the wheel allows reaches it). `REACH`, the terrain stores, the poi `RANGE`, and in `sky.rs` the sky texture's cover and the rain's `DROP_TILE` (a copy of the shader's) are checked against it when compiling; `ViewSquare::reach` asserts it in debug builds. Past it the window keeps that size and so shrinks on screen, with everything still lined up. The stores also hold whole chunks round the window (`REACH + CHUNK`). Raising it means bigger stores (`TerrainGrid` is about 35 MB at 672²).
- Camera (see `view3d.md`): looks at **sea level** (`ViewGrid::floor`), so it doesn't bob over hills. Tokens rest with their centre on the drawn ground at their own point (`rest_tokens_on_terrain`, owner), so on a slope their uphill side dips into it and their low side lifts a little.
- Poi bases (owner's rule): they make pois stand out, flat rather than the old token's shape. A poi rests like a token, the centre of its base at ground level at the poi itself (owner; resting on the highest ground under the base made pois float on slopes). The newer surface generators keep land gentle enough for the dip and lift at the rim to stay small.
- Token placement reads the owner's `common::SmoothPosition`. Pois only exist as entities while the world map is current (other views draw every token), so the dungeon view never sees them.
- Pois are only on dry land (decided in `shared::poi`). Their `Portal` carries the `SeenPoi` (kind, chunk, position: never the map seed); `site` asks the server to enter its chunk.
- The lights, terrain and edge faces are hidden (not despawned) in other views, so the terrain grid and cut are kept for coming back.
- Performance (native dev build, shown in the HUD; 2026-11-09, terrain in chunks): a step fills ~2 ms and cuts the edge faces, rivers and roads in ~2–4 ms, building no chunks (it was ~20 ms with the window cut on the CPU, much more in a browser). Crossing into new chunks fills and builds a row of them. A full refill (new hour, weather, style, or a clearing moving on entering a world map chunk) is ~55 ms plus ~4.5 ms to build all ~170 chunks. A first turn to 45° fills the corner chunks once (~100 ms).
- Vegetation added about 0.4 ms to the cut while walking (13.4 to 13.8 ms, single readings); its cost on the GPU has not been measured.
- Edge faces, rivers and roads use `NoFrustumCulling` because their meshes are replaced after the bounds are computed; terrain chunks are culled, and lose their `Aabb` when rebuilt so it is worked out again. `HeightCache` is inserted by commands at startup, so systems take it as `Option` for the first frame.
- In `common::ClickMode::Teleport`, a click on the map (not a token) sends `DevTeleport` instead of `TravelTo` (dev tools: only the embedded server allows it; through walls in a site, but onto a walkable cell). Uses `connection::ToServer`.
- Shader uniforms must be a multiple of 16 bytes, or WebGL 2 (the web build) refuses the pipeline and the game quits on its first frame; native is fine either way, so nothing else catches it. Pad a short struct with unused `f32`s, the same in the Rust struct and the shader (see `project.md`).
