# client: dom

The interface as markup: HTML's look with XML's closing rule, no CSS and no scripts. A template is checked strictly, built into Bevy UI nodes, and changed by id. The whole plan, the rules of the markup and what is still to come: `docs/context/html-interface.md`. So far it has layout, text, pictures, progress and canvases, and the input rules (what the pointer is on, and events sent up through what holds it), which the old `widgets` read too; and controls (buttons, checkboxes and switches, text boxes, sliders, dropdowns, toggle rows, tabs, key boxes, colour pickers), each shown by one of the old `widgets` inside its element. Panels, dialogs and scroll areas are elements too. The system menu's requesters are the first real screens in markup; the rest follow.

## Public API

- `html!("name", r#"…"#)` (or `include_str!` of a file beside the module): a `Template`, usually a `static`. The source is always part of the program.
- `document(&TEMPLATE)`: the bundle to spawn (as a child of where it goes) for the template to be built there by the end of the frame. `Document(&Template)` is on its root. With `DocumentOf(id)` on the root too, the document is one of several alike (a bag's, a shop's) and every id in it is `form#<id>/name` (`UiId::which` gives the id back when binding). `document_in(&TEMPLATE, element)` builds one inside an element of another document, taking the room there and writing as it does: a page with a template of its own (Settings' Graphics page).
- `Dom` (system parameter): `set_text(id, words)`, `set(id, name, value)`, `set_flag(id, name, on)`, `clear(id, name)`, `raise(id)` (a panel that moves, in front of the others), `start_at(id, at)` (where one just spawned first goes), `copy_template(template, into, index)` (a template of `tr`s goes into a `table`, as its next rows), `empty(id)`, `set_options(id, (value, words) pairs)` (a `select`'s, `tab-bar`'s or `toggle-row`'s options, for lists only known while playing, such as the tabs a role may see; never none; set its `value` after). Ids are as written, `form/name`, and `form/name.<index>` inside a copy of a template. Changes are made in order at the end of the frame, after new documents are built, so a system may spawn a document and fill it in one go. One that can't be made (no such id, a value the attribute can't take) is logged and changes nothing.
- `Template::assert_good()`: every module with templates has a test calling it for each.
- `Theme`: `look(tag, class)`, `set(tag, class, look)`, `has`, `row(size)`, and the fields `rows` and `footer_button` (sizes controls take by themselves); `Theme::parchment()` is the game's. `Look`: text size, colour, margin, gap, background.
- `Pictures::name(name, handle)`: a picture made in code, for `<img src="picture:<name>"/>`.
- `Element { tag }` (component, on every element), `Canvas` (on a `canvas`: its owner adds what it draws), `Inert` (on an `inert` element; nothing reads it until the hit test of PR 2).
- `Tag`, `Attrs`, `Length`, `Align`, `VAlign`, `Checked`, `CheckedNode`, `MarkupError`, `Place`.
- `UiEvent { id, value }` (message): a control was used. Read them in `Update` and match on the id (`event.id == UiId::new("connect", "go")`). `UiValue`: `Pressed` (button), `Flag(bool)` (checkbox, switch), `Text(String)` (text box), `Number(f32)` (slider, between its `min` and `max`), `Choice { index, value }` (dropdown, toggle row, tabs, colour picker), `Key(Option<Binding>)` (key box). The element's attributes are kept as the widget now is (`value`, `checked`), so `client.ui.html` shows them.
- Surfaces: `<panel title>` is a parchment panel where it is written; with `moveable` it is a moveable panel (title bar, close button) starting at its `left` and `top`, and with `grip` a grip panel (also by `right` and `bottom`). `<dialog title>` is a requester over everything, its `<footer>` the row of buttons; `modal` makes it a big layer: its layer's share of the window, or the `width` and `height` it gives itself (one left out fits what is in it), with its `title` in a row along the top and, with `closable`, a close button there whose id is `<form>/close` (a press on it is `ButtonPressed`, and `UiValue::Dismissed` by that id). `depth` is the layer. `<scroll max-height>` (or `height`; pixels, or `%` of the window's height) scrolls what is in it; `auto` scrolls by itself, `scrollbars="always"` keeps its bar on show (`AlwaysBars`); with `fills` it takes the room left in what holds it instead of a height (a page of a dialog with a `height`). Show and hide a panel or dialog with `dom.set_flag(id, "hidden", …)`. A panel that moves follows its `title` whenever it changes, and until it has first been placed (the frame after it is built) its `left` and `top`: so one of several alike (a bag, a shop) is spawned and then given its title and its place in a cascade through `Dom`, in the same system. A `left` and `top` both in `%` are a share of the window across and down (`PanelPlace::Share`), as a place the player left a panel at is saved. Spawn a document that is only a panel that moves on `top_strip()`, so its own node takes no room over the view. `UiValue::Dismissed`: a press outside a dialog. `UiValue::Closed`: a moving panel was closed by its button and has gone; its owner then despawns the document.
- Changing a control from code: `dom.set(id, "value", …)`, `dom.set_flag(id, "disabled" | "checked" | "selected", on)`, `dom.set_text(id, …)` for a button's words, `dom.set(id, "class", "main")` / `dom.clear(id, "class")` for how much a button stands out. A choice's `value` is the chosen option's own `value`, or its words if it has none.
- `Hit` (resource, `PreUpdate`, `DomInput::Find`): `target`, the thing in front at the pointer (an element, or a `Solid` node), `chain` (the target, then everything it is in, outwards) and `at`; `first(pick)` finds the nearest of the chain that `pick` takes. Everything asking what the pointer is on reads this, so only one thing is ever in front.
- `Solid` (component): on a node that isn't an element, it can be the target and hides what is behind it. Elements always can. The old widgets' `Pressable`, `ButtonState` and panel parts require it.
- `Hovered` (component): on each element of the chain, and on nothing else.
- Events, sent to the target and then up through what holds it until an observer stops one (`event.propagate(false)`): `Press`, `Release`, `Click { count }` (2 for a double click), `ContextMenu` (right button), `Wheel { lines }`; and `PointerEnter` / `PointerLeave`, to one element only. Watch with `commands.entity(element).observe(|click: On<Click>| …)`.
- `PointerCapture(Option<Entity>)` (resource): set it for that entity to be the target wherever the pointer goes, until the left button is let go.
- `DomInput::Find` / `Send` (system sets, `PreUpdate`): `Find` is before `WidgetSystems::Presses`, and `Send` between that and `WidgetSystems::Front`.
- `Finder` (system parameter): `target_at(at)`, `chain_of(target)`, for asking about another point.

## Notes

- Our own parser, not a library: the dialect is small and strict, ids must be `&'static str` slices of the source for `UiId`, and errors want a line and column.
- An `id` needs a form: a `<form id="…">` round it or its own `form="…"`. A `form` element has no `UiId` of its own; `form`, `template` and `canvas` must have an `id`.
- A template's ids are checked apart from the document's (each copy gets them numbered), and a `template` is never laid out.
- Words: runs of spaces become one, none at either end, and words made only of spaces are dropped, so the gap between things in a line is the look's `gap` (4 px), not a space in the markup.
- Block elements (`div`, `form`) stack; the rest flow in a wrapping row. `br` and `hr` are as wide as what they are in, so they take a whole line of a flow.
- Tables are a grid whose rows and cells are all children of the table (a grid only places its own children): a `tr` is a strip behind its cells, and hiding it hides them (`restyle` looks at a changed row's cells again).
- `progress` is built as a bar or a spinner by whether it has a `value` in the markup; setting or clearing `value` later doesn't switch between them.
- Changing `class` later changes an element's own look, but not the size or colour of words already inside it.
- `restyle` is the one place a `Node` is made, at spawning and on any change, so markup and `Dom` can't disagree.
- A panel that moves and a dialog are not inside their document: the widgets spawn them at the top, in their own layers, with the element inside and their id on the widget, as controls'. They carry `SurfaceOf(document)` and are despawned when the document is. A scroll area is spawned where it is written, with its element inside it. A panel's and a dialog's `title` is their heading, not a tooltip.
- Moving a screen to markup without changing what it does: write its structure as a template, then give each built widget the components the screen's code already knows it by, found by id in a small system on `Added<UiId>` (`UiId::parts()` gives the form and name): see `system_menu/requesters.rs`. Words that change are set with `Dom::set_text`, and what shows or can be pressed with `Dom::set_flag` (`hidden`, `disabled`), only when it changes: hide a control by its element, not its widget, so its room goes too. This keeps presses arriving in the frame they always did (`ButtonPressed`, in `PreUpdate`); `UiEvent`s arrive a frame later, which suits new code.
- A dialog's or moving panel's element is always laid out, whatever its `hidden` says: only its widget is hidden, so code that shows the widget itself (the menu's `Layer`s) shows what is in it. A dialog's words are the menus' size (`TextSize::Large`).
- Spacing belongs to what holds things, not to the things (owner's principle of automatic layout, 2026-11-11): whatever is stacked in a `div`, `form`, `panel`, `scroll` or `dialog` is 8 apart, and whatever flows in a row 4; nothing has a margin of its own in the theme. `div class="tight"` is 4 apart, `div` / `dialog class="sections"` 24 (a menu's sections), `span class="joined"` and `div class="joined"` touching. A hidden thing takes no gap. `margin` attributes are for the odd case only.
- Sizes are the theme's, not the markup's (owner, 2026-11-11): a control's widget is as wide as its element, which is as wide as what holds it when stacked (a `div`, a dialog) and as itself in a row, so buttons fill their column with no `width`. A control is at least as tall as the theme's row for the writing round it (`Theme::rows`: 38 where it is the menus' size), an icon button a square of that, and a button in a dialog's `footer` at least `Theme::footer_button` wide (120), so a footer's buttons match. Give a `width` or `height` only for what is particular to one screen (a paragraph's wrap width, the Online box beside its gear).
- `align` on an element also sets which side each line of its words is against (and of the words in what it holds, until another `align`), so wrapped lines are centred with `align="center"`.
- Things that flow in a row (`p`, `span`, cells) are never wider than what holds them, so long words wrap. `nowrap` keeps a row on one line: a control that fills (a text box, or a button with `width="100%"`) then takes what the others leave, as they keep their own size (a box with a button beside it).
- `fills`: an element takes the room left in what it is stacked in, and what is in it may scroll inside it (a page under its tabs). What is in a dialog given a `height` fills that height the same way.
- A `footer` may be in any dialog: in a `modal` one it is a row at the right of where it is written. Its buttons share the footer's minimum width wherever it is.
- `scroll` is as wide as what holds it, so rows in it can fill it (`widgets::spawn_wide_scroll_area`).
- A screen partly moved: an element can be a place for code to fill as it always did (Settings' pages are `div`s that the old page-filling systems spawn their rows into), so a screen's shell can be markup before all its contents are.
- A control is two entities: its element (layout, attributes) and, inside it, the widget that shows it (`Control` and `ControlOf` join them). The id is on the widget, as widgets' always were, so the debug API's `client.ui.*` and anything else finding widgets by `UiId` works on controls unchanged; `Dom` finds the element from it. Every control needs an `id`, and an `input` its `type`.
- `<object type="ability" for="<name>"/>` shows the ability of that name as the Abilities page does, with an `AbilityCell` on it. Names and looks come from `KnownAbilities` (a resource: `set(name, AbilityThing)`), which `abilities` fills for the body the character is in; a name nobody has given shows nothing. The plan's `background-color`, `class="active"` and `class="enabled"` aren't read: the look given says all of it.
- Items in text: any writing in markup (written in a template, or set with `Dom::set_text`) that has an item written into it, `${item:<uuid>}`, shows the item there among the words (`build::has_items`; the words' entity gets `widgets::item_text`, and the old widget's systems draw it), named on hovering. Writing without one is a plain `Text` as before. Turning this off for an element, and copying the text form, aren't built: nothing wants them yet.
- For other modules' tests: `dom::test_app()` (an app that builds documents) and `dom::test_find(app, id)`, only when testing.
- Two looks added for the pieces over the game's view (owner, 2026-11-12): `Look::tip`, a tooltip's box (its edge, the room inside it and its shadow: `span class="tip"`, the hover box), and `Look::icons`, writing that falls back to the icon font for symbols (`p class="status"`, white over the view, and `status-warning`, the same in red: the status text). `img class="gold"` tints an `icon:` picture gold. `TextStyle::icons` carries the font down, and `build::font_of` gives the font.
- A top-level document (`document`) is laid out by the node it is given. For pieces over the game's view, give it `top_strip()` or `bottom_strip()`: as wide as the window along that edge, with no height, and the pieces placed from it by `top` or `bottom`. Never a node (or a form that `fills`) over the whole window: a form is an element, so it would be in front of the view and take every press, and even a plain node there is the thing in front for the searches that take any node (tooltips, a held thing, hold buttons). #424's hover box did both, and clicks on the map and every `HoverTip` under it stopped until the strips (2026-11-12). The form in a strip has no height, so the pointer is never on what it holds: a piece that takes the pointer is written with no form round it and its own `form="…"` (the vitals ball).
- A progress bar's colours are the theme's (`Theme::progress`) unless its look has its own (`Look::bar`): `progress class="loading"` is the loading screen's, gold on dark with pale writing.
- `popup` is a dark box in front of the panels, placed by its `left` and `top` (in the interface's pixels; change them with `Dom::set`), taking presses: a choice to make where the pointer is, or more about something. A padlock at its top right locks it open (`Popup::locked`; icons `delapouite/padlock-open` and `plain-padlock`). It closes on Esc (taken, so the menus don't open), locked or not, and unlocked also on a left press anywhere but on it: `close_popups` (`PreUpdate`, in `ClosePopups`: after `PointerSystems`, before `WidgetSystems::Presses`) says so by its id as `UiEvent` with `UiValue::Closed`, and its owner despawns its document (a system reading that goes `.after(ClosePopups)`). Locked, it stays through presses elsewhere, as a box of details to keep open, and can be dragged by its top `GRIP` (24 pixels, about a line of its writing) with the mouse or a finger, which changes its element's `left` and `top` (`drag_popups`, just before `close_popups`); nothing shows where to take hold, as the pointer will say by context later (owner, 2026-11-11). A new one is always unlocked.
- `container` is the frame round a grid of things, the same wherever one is (`build/container.rs`): its `title` at the top left, with its `header` (controls, in a row kept to the right) beside it; then what it holds, stacked; then its `footer` last, a row whose things keep to the right, so one with `fills` (the weight) takes the left. `header` is only for a container; `footer` for a container or a dialog. Its parts are found wherever they are written.
- `span class="place"` has a grid's colour behind it: somewhere a held thing can be let go, such as a row of the Nearby panel (its owner tints it while aimed at).
- A button holds words, or one `img` (an icon button, 32 across unless sized; `class="small"` for the small kind). `hold`: pressed only while held.
- A checkbox is just the box: put its words in a `label`; or give it a `label` attribute for the wide kind with its words inside the button. `readonly`: pressing it doesn't tick it (its owner does), and only says `Pressed`. A click on a label presses what it is for (`for`, or the control inside it); for a text box it gives it the keyboard.
- Toggle rows and tab bars hold `option`s, as a `select` does, rather than HTML's radio buttons in a `fieldset`: one shape for every choice among options.
- In a template with a colour (`value="#be3c32"`), write the source as `r##"…"##`: `"#` ends a string written `r#"…"#`.
- A text box's `prefix` is only taken from the template itself (not set later by code), as the widget wants a `&'static str`.
- Not all of `Dom`, the events and the re-exports is used yet: the `#[allow]`s on them go as screens become markup.
- The search drills down: a point that isn't on an element isn't on anything in it, so its insides are never looked at (markup never lays anything out beyond what holds it). Nodes that aren't elements may hold things anywhere (the old widgets do), so everything in them is tried and the one furthest in front wins; that part shrinks as screens become markup. `inert` and hidden things are passed over with all they hold.
- Our own search, not `bevy_picking`'s: its UI backend tries every node in the stack rather than drilling down, takes its own pointers rather than `UiPointer` (the finger or the mouse, in interface pixels, which fake input moves too), and only tells markers apart for the whole interface.
- A press on an element goes no further (`send_events` takes it from the mouse), so the game behind never sees it. One on another `Solid` node is left to whoever owns it, as before. A right press on a `canvas`, `object-grid` or `object-slot` is left too: the place is its owner's, which turns a held item with it (found when the shop's grid became markup).
- A click goes to the nearest thing both the press and the letting go were on. A finger's tap arrives as a left click (`touch`), so it is a `Click` too.
- Not built yet: more than one pointer at once, the capture phase, focus and keys, drag and drop, a long press as `ContextMenu`, and a canvas saying a point isn't on it. Sliders, text boxes, scroll areas, hold buttons and item grids still do their own searches until they become elements.
- Tables: columns are as wide as what is in them; the column of the first cell marked `fills` takes the width the others leave (`InTable::Table`'s `wide`). A `tr` may be written in a `template` as well as a `table`. A `key-box`'s `value` is a binding's saved name, or `Escape`, which can be shown but never bound.
- A markup text box keeps the keyboard while it has it (`TextBox::takes_keys`). A screen whose own code handles Return, Esc or the arrows turns that off when binding the box (the debug menu's filter).
- `valign` is `top`, `middle`, `bottom`, or in a row `stretch`: everything in it as tall as the row, for columns side by side (Character Select). A row is `middle` unless it says.
- `<object type="item" for="<uuid>"/>` shows the item with that id as a grid does (picture or cells, quality's edge, glow, set edge, count), named when the pointer is on it. Its `for` may be set later (`Dom::set`); with none it takes no room. An item the client doesn't know is asked of the server (`common::KnownItems`, as items in text are) and is an empty cell until it answers, or `?` if the server knows nothing of it. `type="ability"` is checked but not drawn yet. An `input`'s `type` and an `object`'s are kept apart by `check`.
- `object-grid` (`type`, `for`, `columns`, `rows`, `grows`, `places="kept"|"packed"`, `stacks`, `compact`) and `object-slot` (`accepts`, `for`, `key`) are places their owner fills, as a `canvas` is (they carry `Canvas`), with their attributes checked. A grid draws itself: its owner puts a `GridShown` on it (`columns`, `rows`, `things`: each a `GridThing` with its item, top left cell, rotation and `dimmed`; `aim`: a `GridAim` of cells to tint green or red; `takes_gifts`), and `draw_object_grids` (`PostUpdate`, in the set `DrawObjects`) draws its cells, the things in them and the aim, again only as far as what is shown changed (new cells for another size, else only the things or only the aim). Any node with a `GridShown` is drawn so, for screens not yet markup. `GridBox(entity)` on the grid names the node of its cells (made again with the size), and each cell of a drawn thing carries `ThingCell { grid, item, rotation, grip }`: the owner, in a system `.after(DrawObjects)`, puts its own markers on them (`Changed<GridBox>`, `Added<ThingCell>`), and still decides what picking up and letting go there do. A slot draws itself the same way from a `SlotShown` (`look`: `SlotLook::Worn`, as in a grid in one cell with its quality's edge, glow and set edge, or `Kept`, its picture whole inside the frame, as in the bin; `icon` for an empty slot; `thing`: a `SlotThing` with its item, `count`, `faded` and `pressable`; `aim`: green or red): `draw_object_slots` sets the node's size, frame and tint, and draws the thing again only when it is another, its cell and letters carrying `ThingCell` with the slot as `grid`. The elements' attributes aren't read into `GridShown` and `SlotShown` yet.
- Holding: `HeldObject` (a resource) is the one thing held, a `HeldThing` (`id`, the `item` it looks like, `rotation`, `grip`, and the `Hand` that holds it: `Mouse` until the button is let go, `Clicked` until the next click, `Finger`, or `Kept` until put back). An owner picks up with `HeldObject::pick_up(thing, picked)` (`picked`: where and when the mouse did, which tells a click from a drag), ends it with `put_back`, and keeps the thing's look current with `keep_up`. `follow_held` (`PreUpdate`, in `HoldObjects`) turns it (right press, a second finger's tap, `Action::TurnItem`), moves it to the pointer or finger (`at`), says what it is over (`over`: `HeldOver { front, cell }`, the node in front of any kind and the grid in front with the cell the pointer is in), and when its hand lets go writes `Dropped { thing, at, over }`; `show_held` draws it above everything. It knows no rules: an owner's system picking up goes `.before(HoldObjects)`, and one working out what a drop would do, or reading `Dropped`, `.after` it. What is held is a `HeldWhat`: an `Item(HeldItem)` (`inventory`'s; `HeldThing::item`) or an `Ability(AbilityThing)` (`abilities`'; `HeldThing::ability`), which has no shape, doesn't turn and is drawn with its middle at the pointer; each owner takes only its own from `Dropped`. The split box and picking up by a held finger are still `inventory`'s, as they are its rules.
- Abilities (`type="ability"`): a list is an `AbilityGridShown` (`columns`, `things`: each an `AbilityThing`, an `Ability` with the `widgets::AbilityLook` its owner gives it), flowing in rows, every one taking one place; a slot is an `AbilitySlotShown` (`thing`, `key`: its key's short name; `auto`: the sliding metal edge; `on`: a toggle's steady green edge; `queued`: the chasing stripes; `shade`; `aim`). Both draw themselves in `DrawObjects`. Each drawn ability carries `AbilityCell { of, ability }`, and a slot's parts its owner may want carry `SlotPart` (`On`, `Queued`, `Shade`: a node at the slot's bottom whose height the owner sets), for the owner to mark or size.
