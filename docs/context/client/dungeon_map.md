# client: dungeon_map

The dungeon (and building) view: `shared::dungeon` cells drawn in 3D through `view3d`'s camera, with the same tokens as the world map. Shown while `GameView::Dungeon` is current (inside a site, `CurrentDungeon`). Draws what the player, or anything with `SharesSight`, can see (or glimpse), including the level's stairs as 3D models and thin 3D walls, doors and windows, and what they remember (seen before, out of sight now) dim, grey and half see-through; hides tokens on cells nobody sees; lights it from `LightSource`s. Only cells the server has sent are drawn. Walls and floors use AngbandTk tile pictures chosen by the kind of place. Left-click a seen or remembered walkable cell to travel there.

- `crates/client/src/dungeon_map/walls.rs`: `build_wall` draws a wall cell in four quarters, each with its piece of floor (like the nearest open cell, stopping at the wall's foot: `Patch`, `wall_foot`; in `Natural` places `fade_wall` fades the wall's picture across it, `BLEND_WIDTH` 0.25 of a cell), up to two faces (`face_at`: where they stand, from `WallShape::reach`) and its top. A quarter shows if an open cell round that corner of the cell is seen or remembered, so the far side of a wall and the inside of solid rock stay dark; a face if a viewer also stands in front of it. - `crates/client/src/dungeon_map/liquid.rs`: water and lava as channels cut into the floor, drawn in quarters like walls. `outline_at` (the `Outline` of a quarter from the four cells round its grid corner; walls count as liquid where liquid reaches them, and a wall's quarter gets liquid up to its foot): `Round`, `AlongEast` / `AlongSouth` (half a cell wide, `CHANNEL_HALF`), `Notch` (a round bite of floor at the corner), `Full`. `build_cell` draws a liquid cell; `Quarter` draws a quarter: surface (`SURFACE_DROP` below the floor), for what is in sight the bed and sides in the floor's picture (`surfaces_near`), and the floor beside it. `Bed`, `bed_at`: a narrow cell (floor on two opposite sides, `is_narrow`) is half a cell deep, sloping to `FULL_DEPTH` only at seams with a wall or a liquid cell that isn't narrow; anything else is `FULL_DEPTH` all over. `opacity(surface)`: water 0.35, lava solid. `depth_shade` (sides, and walls under liquid: evenly darker from the floor down: 20% at the floor, 40% at half a cell, 60% at a full cell) and `bed_shade` (20% darker again, so 60% and 80%, following a sloping bed). - `crates/client/src/dungeon_map/holes.rs`: `build_hole` draws a `Cell::Hole` in quarters: what is left of the floor (`remains`: its outline and the edge along the hole), the hole's side below that edge, and a black base. ## Notes

- Towns (owner's rules): fixed full light for now (`LightGrid::filled`, and brighter ambient on tokens), to be replaced by positional lights with decorations; every cell is known, so out of sight is grey, not missing. Memory of `Surface::Town` is opaque rather than half see-through: a whole town would otherwise have to sort. Walls are `WALL_OUT` from cell centres, and the half cell beyond them is always the land's ground (even beside a road or garden), so it shows between buildings too. Roofs slope up across the outermost cells only, fading to black, flat black inside. Doors fill their wall cell with the kind's picture (a house's is plain); windows are black panes with nothing behind. Clicking a door (`picking::door_front`) travels to the cell it faces, and the HUD names the building from there. Hedges: 20% of a cell thick, tall and inset; they block nothing; arches are 1.5 cells tall. A stretch of 3 or more has an arch half the time (by a hash of its first cell, so all clients agree), never at its ends; a door facing a hedge always gets one and the stretch no other.
- Farms (owner's rules): outside cells have fixed full light for now (`LightGrid::light_outside`), like a town's, while the house is lit by lanterns as usual; only what is in sight is drawn of crops, never memory, and ruined fields have none. Crops are upright pictures in a `#`, two rows each way per cell, 6, 12 or 18 pixels tall; a stage is looks only, chosen here, so the server never hears of it. Time of year is to decide it later. Demo key F moves all fields on a stage; `GAME_FIELDS` sets one.
- Outside (`Cell::Ground`, owner's rule): it is the world outside, so it shares the land and weather of the site's place on the world map (`WorldReturn`). The ground is one colour over the whole site, and precipitation falls only on outside cells in sight, at the world map's density and with its rules for rain, sleet, snow and hail. Clouds aren't shown here.
- Grid cell `(x, y)` is centred on `(x × CELL_SIZE, 0, y × CELL_SIZE)`; walls are `WALL_HEIGHT` tall, thin, down the middle of their cells (their rules are in `shared::dungeon`), but only their seen sides are drawn, with their tops in the quarters shown.
- Both sides of a wall look the same (owner's): if any side someone sees is turned away from the camera, every seen side of that stretch of wall (each way it runs, at a corner) is see-through, and a viewer in line with a wall, side-on to it, sees both its sides. So side-on walls are see-through whichever way the camera is.
- Wall rule (owner's): only sides someone sees are drawn. A seen side facing the camera is solid; one facing away (the wall stands between the camera and what is seen) is drawn 50% see-through, from behind (the see-through material doesn't cull back faces). Facing is measured from the camera's position, not its direction, so as the camera turns a side flips only when the camera passes its plane; this is the same test as back-face culling, so solid sides are always front faces. Out of sight: remembered sides facing the camera are drawn grey (see-through ones are left out, so memory stays quiet); never-seen ones not at all. A thick wall (two rows) or solid rock shows black between its faces: those quarters can never be seen.
- A quarter can show because of an open cell round the wall's end or a column, with a face in it out of sight (the viewer is on the other side). If it faces the camera it is drawn as memory, even if never seen (the wall is known, and it gives nothing away; a hole looks wrong), and so are that quarter's floor and top if it has no face in sight.
- Nothing is drawn on the floor under a wall's foot, so see-through and remembered walls show a dark band there (wider on natural walls, whose foot is wider). Under a solid wall the footprint of the quarters in sight is filled (`fill_foot`), in the wall's picture at its top's shade: the curve lets the camera see in under the wall from the side, and it would otherwise show the background as a thin black slit. Doors stop the floor at their sides; natural windows' floor runs on under the columns. The fade reaches at most `BLEND_WIDTH` so it is gone by the edge of a cell where a wall ends, and matches the next wall cell where it carries on; it stays inside wall, door and window cells, and doesn't cross into open cells.
- Light is worked out on the grid and baked into vertex colours (unlit materials): `picture × (ambient + (1 − ambient) × light)`, so full light shows the picture as drawn. Floors take corner-averaged light; walls blend between their cell's corners, except across the wall itself, so each side of it is lit by its own side. Light colours add up (clamped), so coloured lights work already; all are white for now. Stairs take their cell's light, times their shading (applied after the ambient, so the hole can be really dark).
- Each picture covers one cell (32 pixels per cell); parts smaller than a cell (steps) show part of it, so the texture keeps its scale.
- Remembered surfaces use grey copies of the pictures, made on the CPU once each picture loads (until then they show in colour), at an even `MEMORY_SHADE` rather than their light, half see-through (real blending, with floors' `depth_bias` keeping them behind walls; floors show through walls). `MEMORY_SHADE` is kept low so memory isn't brighter than cells in sight in the dull light.
- Pictures are clamped, not repeated: every piece keeps its picture coordinates within its cell, using its position there, so it lines up with the next cell. A wall carried on under liquid and the sides of a channel start the picture again from its top at the floor, so they repeat rather than stretch.
- Liquid (owner's rules): narrow where it has floor on two opposite sides, and half a cell deep there; a narrow channel's bed stays flat from cell to cell, and reaches a full cell deep only where it meets a wider liquid cell or a wall. Doors and windows count as floor, so liquid stops short of them. Liquid cells never pass their surface to neighbouring floors (`floor_quarter` skips them). In a wall's cell the liquid and bed run on under the wall's foot, unclipped, so they show under a see-through wall; a channel stopping at the wall's centre line (the far side dry) is closed there in the wall's picture (`close_behind_wall`), since at a wall's end, where the wall is narrower than the channel, that shows through the curve. Remembered liquid is only its flat outline: blended meshes don't sort against themselves, so bed and sides would muddle, and memory stays quiet. No fade across liquid (owner: it doesn't need blending).
- Holes (owner's rules): geometry, not pictures. The client doesn't have the level below, so through a hole it is only dark. A wall beside a hole keeps its floor patch, as a ledge. Ground eaten from a ruined building is plain `Cell::Ground`: biome colour, weather and the same light as any other cell, checked in screenshots.
- Natural shape is chosen by the theme for now; the generators may choose it per tile later, with more variations (door tops, window kinds). Material relief is only the wandering surface and shading: the materials are unlit, so no normal maps.
- Stairs are drawn from `CurrentDungeon::stairs`, not as tokens, in the style and running the way the server worked out. Models stay within 0.4 of a cell of the centre, leaving a border of floor (rope pegs stand on it). Spirals and ladders are placed the same on both levels (top on the `-descends` side, where the upper level's floor is); turning stairs have both ends on one side, so they open towards `+descends` going up and `-descends` going down, each onto the floor of its own level. Near sides of a shaft are culled, so the model shows. Treads are the floor's picture brightened (`TREAD_TINT`), step sides the wall's, darker; wood is `Surface::Door`'s picture, running along one plank (`PLANK_U`, `PLANK_V`); rope is one spot of it, tinted paler (`ROPE_UV`, `ROPE_TINT`). Models going up are built taller than going down, so they climb at a believable rate, and cut off at `STAIRS_UP_TOP` (`Model::ceiling`), which is above the walls. Stairs going up have no handrails (owner's). Clicks only land on the floor of the stairs' cell, not the model standing on it, so what is behind can be clicked (owner's; may change back). Rungs and knots are `UP_SPACING` further apart going up. Stairs hiding a viewer (`hides_viewer`) draw what stands above the floor half see-through (`StairsThroughPart`, one mesh per surface, back faces culled, faces not sorted) ; the floor, hole and anything below stay solid. The owner's rule is the same as for walls in the way, but tested by line of sight to the viewer rather than by which way a side faces. Step fronts are a fixed amount darker than treads whichever way they face (`FRONT_SHADE`; `front_tint` undoes `facing_shade`). Rails have a top and a middle bar. Going down only (owner's): the steps have no rails; a spiral's top step is a landing of twice the angle, with a rail round the top of the hole beside the landing and across its far edge to the column; turning stairs have a rail round the hole's top leaving the way onto the top step open. Depth shades are linear, so small numbers (`MODEL_DEEPEST_SHADE` 0.03) are what show as about 80% darker on screen.
- Tokens can't use baked light, so they get real lights: a `PointLight` (no shadows) `TOKEN_LIGHT_HEIGHT` above each light source, range (radius + 1) cells, plus a dim ambient. Tuned by eye. The player arriving by stairs stands on them (the token isn't raised or lowered).
- Sight and light use `MapPosition` (whole cells). Drawing (and hiding tokens, and click picking) uses glimpses, which are more generous than the game's symmetric sight; light keeps the symmetric rule.
- The see-through mesh is one mesh with `AlphaMode::Blend`, so its faces don't sort against each other; with one picture at 50% this hasn't shown.
- Clicks: `TravelTo`; `player` finds the route (`shared::pathfinding`) and travels at 12 cells/s here.
- Rebuilding everything (with sight) is about 1 ms for a 50 × 66 castle floor; it happens every frame while the camera moves (dragging, travelling). Bigger levels may need to rebuild only what changed.
- Ctrl+click on the map (not a token) sends `DevTeleport` instead of `TravelTo` (dev tools: only the embedded server allows it; through walls in a site, but onto a walkable cell). Alt+click sends `DevSetCell` (floor → wall → door → window → floor), to try wall shapes. Uses `connection::ToServer`.
