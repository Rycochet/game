# client: item_art

Pictures of items: each look is drawn once in 3D, off screen, a page of them at a time, and kept as an image of its own for the interface to show.

## Files

- `crates/client/src/item_art/mod.rs`: `ItemArtPlugin`, the constants every model shares, the module list. Beside it: `look.rs` (`Look`, `RingLook`, `look_of`), `pictures.rs` (`ItemPictures`, `ItemPicture`, `Waiting`), `drawing.rs` (models' parts, the lights, waiting for shaders), `draw.rs` (`fill_pictures`, `draw_pictures`: starting, filling, drawing and finishing pages), `parts.rs` (`parts_of`: a look's model from its builder), `batch.rs` (`Batch`: one page being drawn, its slot, two images, cells and edge materials), `sheet.rs` (`Shelves`: placing pictures on a page in rows; unit tests), `spawn.rs` (lights, a page's two cameras, each picture's model and edge square), `edge.rs` (`EdgeMaterial`, `assets/shaders/picture_edge.wesl`), `copy.rs` (copying pictures off the page on the graphics card), `samples.rs` (looks the same every time: the warm-up while loading, and for timing), `scale.rs` (more pixels on a larger interface), `debug_api.rs` (`client.time_pictures`, `client.picture_times`).

## Public API

- `ItemArtPlugin`.
- `Look::Hands { hands: HandsLook, quality }`, `HandsLook::new(form, digits, cells)`: a pair of hand pieces; how many digits each hand has shows.
- `Look::Shoulders { shoulders: ShouldersLook, quality }`, `ShouldersLook::new(form, cells)`: a pair of shoulder pieces.
- `Look::Torso { torso: TorsoLook, quality }`, `TorsoLook::new(form, cells)`: a torso piece, as worn.
- `Look::Arms { arms: ArmsLook, quality }`, `ArmsLook::new(form, cells)`: an arm piece, as worn.
- `Look::Legs { legs: LegsLook, quality }`, `LegsLook::new(form, cells)`: a leg piece, as worn.
- `Look::Waist { waist: WaistLook, quality }`, `WaistLook::new(form, cells)`: a waist piece, as worn.
- `Look::Feet { feet: FeetLook, quality }`, `FeetLook::new(form, cells)`: a pair of foot pieces, as worn.
- `Look::Footwear { footwear: FootwearLook, quality }`, `FootwearLook::new(form, cells)`: a pair of footwear, as worn.
- `Look::Neck { neck: NeckLook, quality }`, `NeckLook::new(form, cells)`: a neck piece, as worn.
- `Look::Head { head: HeadLook, quality }`, `HeadLook::new(form, cells)`: a head piece, as worn.
- `Look::Body { kind }`: a bare body, for kinds with a frame; worn things will be added to it for a character's picture.
- `Look`, `RingLook`, `BladeLook`: everything a picture shows, and so what pictures are kept by. Ids stay out of it, so things that look the same share a picture. `look_of(item)`: an item's, if it has a form. `Look::cells`, `longest`, `picture_size(pixels)`: its proportions.
- `ItemPicture { look, pixels }` (component, `pixels` along the longest side): on an `ImageNode`, it shows that picture. `widgets::item_picture` is the usual way.
- `ItemPictures` (resource): `picture(look, pixels, images)` gives the image straight away, empty until drawn, and the same one if asked again. Asking early (as `inventory` does for what is carried) gets it drawn sooner.

## Depends on

- `shared::item`, `shared::item_form`, `shared::blade`, `shared::armour`, `shared::body`, `shared::creature`, `shared::material`, `shared::rng`. No other module.

## Used by

## Notes

- Detail: pictures are asked for at twice the pixels they are shown with, sharp up to two screen pixels per interface pixel. Beyond that (a large `UiScale` on a scaled screen) `ItemPictures` draws them with `detail` times the pixels asked for (at most `MOST_DETAIL`), and `follow_interface_scale` gives those on show the new ones. The black edge goes by the pixels asked for, so it stays as thick on the screen. Debug Item Pictures' own requests only pick it up when they next change.
- Colours and finishes are the client's, and meshes aren't rolled: everything to do with what a thing is comes from its saved form, so the same item looks the same anywhere, though pixels may differ between graphics cards.
- Pages (owner's idea, 2026-11-03): waiting pictures are laid out in rows on a page (`PAGE_PIXELS` square, larger only for a picture that needs it), with a gap round each, and their models built and placed there for up to `BUILD_TIME` a frame, so a panel full of new things doesn't stall a frame: what is built in a frame is one page, and up to `PAGES` are drawn at once. One orthographic camera draws every model on a page (its own render layer from 16 up, with the view, lights and multisampling a picture of its own had; models are scaled down to `PAGE_UNIT` a pixel so a page stays in front of it). Then a second camera (the next layer, no multisampling) draws a square per picture with `EdgeMaterial` onto a second image of the page. Each picture is then copied off the page into its own image on the graphics card (`PageCopies`, a render-world system after drawing), so everything that shows pictures, auras included, still has one image per picture. The rest wait for a page to be free. Building models is quick (300 took 0.09 s in the debug build); waiting on the graphics card is what takes the time.
- Something new to draw isn't drawn until the graphics card's program for it has compiled, so a page's cameras stay until `StillCompiling` is 0 and then `FRAMES_ALIVE` frames more, copying in each but the last. Without that the first pictures after starting come out empty. Same idea as Bevy's loading screen example. To have the programs ready, `samples::warm_up` asks for one picture of each kind of look at startup, and `inventory` asks for everything carried as soon as it arrives.
- Three directional lights (owner's idea: strongest from the top right, so no face is black and metal has something to catch), shared and there only while drawing, as Bevy allows 10 directional lights in all. Bevy skips a light for a view not on its layers, so these and the world's don't mix, and each camera has its own `AmbientLight`.
- A cut gem is one mesh drawn twice: inside of the far faces opaque and darker, then the near faces 40% see-through (owner's request). Being convex, its faces can't sort wrongly. No refraction: it costs a lot, and flat faces of varied shade read well at this size.
- Better quality means more contrast between faces and a sharper shine, except for gems, where it is the other way round (owner's choice: the softer, broader shine looks shinier).
- Every picture has a black edge (owner's request), 4 of its pixels, so 2 as shown: every pixel whose middle is within that of one the model covers at least half of is black, under the model (round and hard: owner's choice). It goes by what the camera sees, so any model gets it with nothing of its own. A model must leave that much room inside `VIEW_WIDTH`, and must stay inside it, or it would show in its neighbour's picture.
- Pictures are drawn at twice the size they are shown, which is what that thickness assumes.

- A picture has the proportions of what its item takes up, not turned: square for gems and rings, long for weapons. It must be asked for at 64 pixels per cell of its longest side for its black edge and margin to be right. A picture turned a quarter is laid out the other way round about the same middle (`widgets::picture_node`). Where one cell shows it, `widgets::picture_in_square` keeps its proportions.
- A weapon points up with its edge to the right, and its length is squeezed to fit but its width much less (one cell shows at most 15 cm across), or a pike would be a black line. It is placed by its bounds, so anything rolled fits, which a test checks. Lengths in `BladeLook` are to the nearest centimetre, so that nearly the same weapons share a picture. No fine curves on heads: at this size their outlines are what show.
- Size isn't in `Look`: a picture fills what the item takes up, at any size. Only sizes against each other show: a setting against its ring, a gem against its setting, which is how a gem too big looks it.
- Metal is half metallic rather than fully: fully metal shows only what it reflects, and nothing is round it to reflect. Patterns are cheap: twisted turns the cross-section on its way round, hammered tilts each small face's normal, engraved darkens bands of faces.
- Pictures are kept in memory for good, about 16 KB each at one cell, and drawn again next time the game starts. Keeping them on disk was measured and not done: see `optimisations.md`.
- Armour is built in millimetres in place on `shared::body::Frame`, with parts by what they are made of (`armour::Stuff`), so sets and the character picture can reuse the same builders. `body.rs`'s `loft`, `limb`, `blob` and `mesh_of` are shared within `item_art` for this, and `trunk_half` / `trunk_depth` give the trunk's surface as drawn, for armour to keep clear of.
- Shoulders: surfaces are functions of two numbers giving a point and its normal, from which plates, bands of lames or lamellae, scales, studs and mail lines are made. The cop is a dome over the shoulder joint, lames are bands round the upper arm flaring out, wings follow the trunk 45 mm out; rigid pieces sit 35 mm out from the skin (outside breastplate and gorget), hanging guards 28 mm out from the arm, capes 22 mm. Styles shape plate as on gauntlets. Japanese lamellae are lacquered black; ailettes are painted in their heraldry. The picture shows each piece of a pair turned a little to show its outside, side by side; capes and the wing as worn.
- Hands: each hand hangs from the wrist, palm to the thigh, thumb forwards. Plates are thin shells (`plate`) from rows of points with their normals; lames step out 1.4 mm over the next so their edges show; mail and quilting are shaded in alternate strips. A cuff is 28 mm out from the forearm, over a vambrace. Styles change plate shapes: flutes (Gothic, Maximilian), points and cusps (Gothic, Elven), square cuffs (Kastenbrust), thick plates and a heavy knuckle bar (Dwarven), etched bands (Greenwich). The picture lays both down, fingers to the right, the right hand above with its thumb up, tipped back a little, and fits them by their bounds, as a test checks.
- Shader uniforms must be a multiple of 16 bytes, or WebGL 2 (the web build) refuses the pipeline and the game quits on its first frame; native is fine either way, so nothing else catches it. Pad a short struct with unused `f32`s, the same in the Rust struct and the shader (see `project.md`).
