# shared: body

Where things are on a standing body, in millimetres: the frame every worn thing's model is built on, so one model makes both its own picture and a character wearing it. Not rules for wearing (`creature`, `wear`).

## Files

- `Hair { style: HairStyle, beard: Beard }`, `Hair::NONE`: hair to draw on a head (`item_art`).
- `crates/shared/src/body.rs`: `Frame` (the figures for each body shape: `HUMAN`, the person every frame is measured against, and `GIANT`; the first peoples' come from their proportions), `Frame::of`, `Frame::joint`, `Joint`, `Side`. Tests.
- `crates/shared/src/body/hair.rs`: `HairStyle` (Bald, Short, Long, TiedBack, Braids, Topknot, Crest, Curly), `Beard` (None, Short, Full, Long, Braided, Forked), each with `name`, and `Hair { style, beard }` (`NONE`): the shapes hair is drawn in. Which a creature has is a look (`sheet`).
- `crates/shared/src/body/parts.rs`: `Parts` (what a body has beyond a person's: `Snout`, ears and their `Ears` shape, `Horns`, tusks, tail and its `Tail` shape, `Feet`, `Wings`, a mane and tail hair (a horse's mane and long tail, a cow's switch; a centaur keeps its horse's tail hair), a cow's dewlap and udder, a hen's comb and wattles; shapes as kinds, sizes as millimetres) and `NONE`.
- `crates/shared/src/body/bipeds.rs`: `Biped` (a name, usual height, proportions and parts), `FIRST_BIPEDS` (the first peoples from the research, each a `CreatureKind` of the same name), `Biped::of(kind)`, `Parts::of(kind)`.
- `crates/shared/src/body/layout.rs`: `Layout`, any body's layout: `Spine` (from the hips' middle to the neck base, `pitch` 0 upright to 90 level, `Station`s at hips, waist, chest, and the trunk's outline as `rings`), `Neck`, `Head` (with an optional `HeadShape`), up to `MAX_LIMBS` `Limb` slots (`Role`, `Girdle`, side, up to `MAX_BONES` `Bone`s, foot) and `Parts`; `Layout::of`, `limb`, `root`, `joints`, `chin`, `Spine::at`, `Spine::neck_root`. Tests in `layout/tests.rs`.
- `crates/shared/src/body/head_shape.rs`: `HeadShape` (a measured head's outline: `pitch` nose down and `HEAD_RINGS` `Ring`s along the head's line from where it sits on the neck, back of the skull to the nose, `belly` below the line and `back` above it), `HeadShape::plain` (an oval head and its snout as rings, for blending), `bounds`. Tests.
- `crates/shared/src/body/measured_heads.rs`: `MeasuredHead`, `WOLF_HEAD`, `COW_HEAD`, `DOG_HEAD`, `HORSE_HEAD`, `MEASURED_HEADS`: heads as `cargo xtask shapes` measures them, in thousandths of their length (`cargo xtask test` checks them); `MeasuredHead::shape(length, pitch)` scales one to ours, at our own carriage.
- `crates/shared/src/body/rings.rs`: `RINGS` (10), `HEAD_RINGS` (8), `Ring` (along from the hips, half width, `belly` in front of the spine and `back` behind it), `from_stations` (rings for a body with no measurements, from its stations, limbs and neck: the six levels its trunk was always drawn through, with rings halfway between). Tests.
- `crates/shared/src/body/measured.rs`: `Measured`, `MeasuredRing`, `COW`, `DOG`, `HORSE`, `MEASURED`: trunks as `cargo xtask shapes` measures them from JALdMIC's MakeHuman animals, copied unchanged in thousandths of the withers' height (`cargo xtask test` checks they still match); `Measured::rings` scales them to one of our beasts.
- `crates/shared/src/body/measured_person.rs`: `MeasuredUpright`, `PERSON_TRUNK`: MakeHuman's base person as `cargo xtask shapes` measures it standing upright (crotch, hip joints, shoulders, neck, and rings with front and back from the line up through the hip joints), in thousandths of its height (`cargo xtask test` checks them); `MeasuredUpright::rings(frame)` shapes any frame's trunk after it. Tests.
- `crates/shared/src/body/beasts.rs`: `Beast` (a name, a `Plan` and `Parts`), `Fourfoot` (a few measurements in millimetres: legs' radii per joint, `fore` and `hind`, and `foot`; its trunk's `measured` shape) and `Bird`, `BEASTS` (horse, wolf, cattle, chicken), `Beast::of(kind)`, `Beast::layout`. In `beasts/build.rs`: layouts from them: a level back over four legs, or a bird's leaning back over two, legs as shares of the height bent at each joint.
- `crates/shared/src/body/biped_view.rs`: `Layout::biped(frame, parts)` and `Layout::frame()`: a `Frame` is the view of an upright layout with two arms and two legs.
- `crates/shared/src/body/taurs.rs`: `Taur` (a name, the `Lower` body: one of `BEASTS` by name or an `Arthropod`; the person's `Proportions` above; `Parts`), `TAURS` (the centaur on the horse, the drider on a spider, the scorpion man on a scorpion), `Taur::of(kind)`, `Taur::layout`; on `Layout`: `upper_body` (the upper body as a layout of its own, in place, arms only) and `worn_frame` (the frame worn things are built on and how far to move them).
- `crates/shared/src/body/taurs/arthropod.rs`: `Arthropod` (height, front body's length and halves, legs' reach and thickness) and its layout: a level front body with four pairs of `JointedLeg`s spread along it (`Girdle::Along`), each rising to its knee, falling out and down to the ground.
- `crates/shared/src/body/blend.rs`: `Layout::blend(a, b, t)`, one body turning into another (`t` thousandths of the way, `BLEND_WHOLE` = 1000); spine (its rings one by one), neck, head and a taur's upper body (`grown` out of the back when the other has none). In `blend/`: `limbs.rs` (pairing on each side by role, then what each becomes, then slot order; bones, ways and girdles blended; limbs with no partner from or to a point), `head.rs` (sizes, and the outline ring by ring, a head without one blended as its `HeadShape::plain`; each end exactly its own), `parts.rs` (sizes blended, snout and horn kinds swapped at nothing halfway, feet, wings, sting and ear and tail shapes at halfway; `scaled`), `frame.rs` (`zip`, `scaled`: frames number by number, for upper bodies). Tests in `blend/tests.rs`.
- `crates/shared/src/body/scaled.rs`: `Layout::scaled(share)` (the same body this many thousandths of its size, on the same ground) and `Layout::at_size(kind)` (a biped's layout scaled from a person's height to its kind's usual one; anything else as it is). Tests.
- `crates/shared/src/body/trig.rs`: sine and cosine by whole degrees from a table, in ten-thousandths, so tilted points agree on both sides.
- `crates/shared/src/body/proportions.rs`: `Proportions` (heads tall, breadth, limbs, arms, legs, beside a person's) and `PERSON`; `Proportions::frame` works a whole frame out from them. Research: `docs/context/research-creatures.md`.

## Public API

- `has_legs(kind)` (`body/limbs.rs`): any limb it stands on; kinds with legs know Kick.
- `has_hands(kind)` (`body/limbs.rs`): arms (not wings), or a person's upper body on a beast's; a kind whose body isn't drawn has none. Mobs with hands open doors.

- `Layout::of(kind) -> Option<Layout>`: kinds drawn so far: on two legs, and the horse and chicken from `BEASTS`; and taurs (centaur, drider, scorpion man) from `TAURS`. The human's (and beggar's) is built from `PERSON.frame()`, the giant's from `GIANT`, the first peoples' from their `Biped`, with `Parts::of(kind)`.
- `Spine { base, pitch, hips, waist, chest, shoulders, neck_base, rings }`; `Ring { along, half, belly, back }`, `RINGS`: the trunk's outline from the rump to the neck's base, the same count on every body. `Measured`, `MeasuredRing`, `MEASURED` (`COW`, `DOG`, `HORSE`). `MeasuredUpright`, `PERSON_TRUNK` (every biped's trunk). `HEAD_RINGS`, `HeadShape { pitch, rings }`, `MeasuredHead`, `MEASURED_HEADS` (`WOLF_HEAD`, `COW_HEAD`, `DOG_HEAD`, `HORSE_HEAD`).
- `Layout { spine, neck, head, limbs, parts, upper }`; `Upper { frame, at }`: a person's upper body (its frame, from the hips up) standing with the middle of its hips at `at` on the lower body; a taur's lower body has no neck or head (length and head 0), the upper body's being its own. `Layout::worn_frame() -> Option<(Frame, [i32; 3])>`: a biped's own frame unmoved, or a taur's upper frame and its offset. `Layout::upper_body()`. `Layout::limb(role, side)`, `limbs()`, `chin()` (the neck's top, by its tilt, from `Spine::neck_root`: the middle of the trunk's last ring), `root(limb)` (where it hangs from), `joints(limb)` (root, then each bone's end); `Spine::at(along)`. `Bone { span, way, radius, end_radius }`: its end is `way` (mm per metre, x outwards) × `span` / 1000 on. `Role::{Arm, WingArm, Leg, Foreleg, Hindleg, JointedLeg}`, `Girdle::{Hips, Shoulders, Along(mm)}`.
- `Layout::blend(&a, &b, t) -> Layout`, `BLEND_WHOLE`: for shapeshifting; each end is exactly its own body. Blend layouts from `Layout::at_size(kind)`, so sizes change too. `Layout::scaled(share)`.
- `Layout::biped(&Frame, Parts)`, `Layout::frame() -> Option<Frame>` (upright, with arms or wing-arms and legs).
- `Frame::of(kind) -> Option<Frame>`: `Layout::of(kind)?.frame()`.
- `Proportions { heads, breadth, limbs, arms, legs }`, `PERSON`, `Proportions::frame()`: `heads` in tenths (a person is 80), the rest in thousandths of a person's.
- `Frame` fields: heights of the main levels from the ground, half widths and depths, limb radii, limb lengths, `arm_spread`.
- `Frame::usual_height(kind)`: how tall a kind on two legs usually stands, in millimetres (its frame is always a person's height).
- `Head { length, half, shape }`: `shape` is `Some(HeadShape)` for an animal with a measured (or, the hen, hand-drawn) head, which is then drawn from its rings and its snout left out; `None` for a person's oval and snout, drawn as always.
- `Parts { snout, snout_length, ears, ear_shape, horns, horn_length, tusks, tail, tail_shape, sting, abdomen, feet, wings, mane, tail_hair, dewlap, udder, comb }` (`abdomen`: a spider's or scorpion's rear body's length; `sting`: the tail curls up over the back), `NONE`, `Ears::{Swept, Upright, Sideways, Dropped}`, `Tail::{Plain, Bushy, Thin, Tufted}`, `Snout::{Flat, Muzzle, Long, Beak}`, `Horns::{None, Back, Bull}`, `Feet::{Plantigrade, Digitigrade, Hooves, Talons}`, `Wings::{None, Arms, Folded}`.
- `Beast { name, plan, parts }`, `Plan::{Fourfoot, Bird}`, `BEASTS`: each a `CreatureKind` of the same name (a test checks). `Parts::of(kind)` covers beasts too.
- `Biped { name, height, proportions, parts }`, `FIRST_BIPEDS`: halfling, dwarf, elf, orc, goblin, kobold, lizardfolk, minotaur and birdfolk. `Biped::of(kind)` finds a kind's by name; `Parts::of(kind)` is its parts, `NONE` for any other kind.
- `Frame::joint(Joint) -> [i32; 3]`: x towards the body's left, y up, z forwards, origin on the ground between the feet.
- `Joint::{Crown, Skull, Chin, NeckBase, Shoulder, Elbow, Wrist, Fingertips, Chest, Waist, Hip, Knee, Ankle, Toes, Heel}`, `Side::{Left, Right}` (`BOTH`, `sign`).

## Notes

- Every frame is at an ordinary person's scale (1750 mm tall), as blade lengths are: an item's size goes on top. So a kobold's frame is its proportions, not its height.
- Arms hang a little out from the sides (`arm_spread` per metre down), so sleeves and gauntlets show in a picture; the elbows are slightly bent, so the forearms hang nearer straight down and a little forwards (`forearm_spread`, `forearm_forward`), and the wrists and fingertips follow the forearm (owner, 2026-11-05, with the shoulders lowered a little).
- Whole millimetres, so weights worked out from it agree on both sides. Figures are first guesses from artists' proportions.
- Frames from `Proportions`: the head's height follows `heads`, the hips (and knees, ankles, feet) `legs`, and the shoulder, chest and waist levels spread over what lies between the neck and the hips, so every level stays in order. A person's proportions give `HUMAN` exactly (tested). The giant keeps its own figures; the kobold's frame comes from its proportions since the first peoples became kinds.
- The general layout (owner, 2026-11-04) is what other body plans, taurs and morphing build on: a spine that tilts, limb slots with roles, head, parts as numbers. A `Frame` is its two-legged view, so the ~290 places armour reads `Frame` stay as they are; a test checks every kind's frame through its layout is the one worked out before. Bipeds are still described by their frame figures (from `Proportions`) and their layout built from them; later body plans build layouts directly. Limbs hang down in the standing body's directions whatever the spine's tilt, so legs stand on the ground. Tail and wing-arms are still in `Parts`.
- The trunk's outline (creature shapes PR 2, `docs/context/creature-shapes.md`): beasts on four legs take their rings from `MEASURED` (the wolf the dog's, the centaur's lower body the horse's), along the spine scaled by their body's length (hips to shoulders) and the rest by their height at the withers, belly and back from their own spine. Bipeds (and a taur's upper body) take theirs from `PERSON_TRUNK` on their frame (creature shapes PR 4): widths and depths at the hips, waist and chest are the frame's, but the front and back are split as measured, so the chest stands forwards of the spine and the back is flatter; the shape fades in from the hip joints to the waist, and the crotch and neck rings are as before. Every other body's rings come from its stations. The stations and frames keep their figures, as worn things are built on them; what is worn round the trunk reads the rings (client `trunk_half`). A ring's `belly` is negative when the whole ring is behind the spine (a rump's tip above a level back). The horse's last two rings climb into the neck, as the meshes' do.
- Beasts' layouts are at their own usual size in millimetres, not a person's scale as bipeds' frames are; their pictures fill themselves. Measurements are first guesses from the research (owner to tweak, as with the bipeds). Necks lean forwards by `Neck::pitch`; a frame needs the spine and neck upright.
- Heads (creature shapes PR 3): the wolf, cattle and horse take their heads from `MEASURED_HEADS` (the wolf the wolf head's, cattle the cow's, the horse its own body's), the hen a hand-drawn `CHICKEN_HEAD` in `beasts.rs`, each scaled to our own length from the back of the skull to the nose (`Fourfoot::head`: length, pitch, outline). Only proportions come from the meshes; the carriage (`Neck::pitch`, `HeadShape::pitch`) is ours: a cow's head low, a wolf's level with its back, a horse's 45° nose down. A head's line starts at the neck's top (`Layout::chin`, the measured `head` joint), its rings running from behind it (the back of the skull) to the nose; a horse's first rings are still in its neck, as its head grows out of it. `Head::length` and `half` of a shaped head are its outline's `bounds`, for what still goes by them. `DOG_HEAD` is measured but unused, for beast peoples' heads (PR 5). Bipeds have no `HeadShape`, so their heads and helmets are as before; PR 5 gives some peoples one, and helmets must then fit it.
- Necks start at `Spine::neck_root`, the middle of the trunk's last ring (the spine's end for a body worked out from its stations, so bipeds are unchanged), and the client lofts a shaped head's neck from that ring, so the trunk carries on into the neck. `Fourfoot::coat` thickens a measured trunk all round (the wolf's fur).
- A taur's upper body is at a person's scale, as a biped's frame is, standing on its beast at the beast's own size: a centaur is a person on a 1.58 m horse, its hips at the withers less a third of the barrel, a third of the barrel in front of the shoulder joints. A frame is only for an upright body without an upper one; armour on a taur goes by `worn_frame`.
- Blending (shapeshifting, owner 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-06): limbs are paired per side, so a blend never needs more slots than the side with more limbs; one with no partner hangs from its girdle and grows from nothing there, left out at size 0, as are bones a partner lacks (grown at the limb's end). A blended bone's way keeps the blended length of the two ways, so a leg swinging forwards doesn't shorten on the way. A taur's upper body grows about its hips, which keep their place over the blended body's shoulders.
