# shared: pathfinding

Finding a way across one level of a site or the world map: the quickest route of legal steps between two points, for the player's clicks now and monsters and spells later. Not between floors.

## Files

- `crates/shared/src/pathfinding.rs`: `Path`, `find_path` (A*), `straighten`, `find_world_path`, `WORLD_ROUTE_REACH`. Tests: straight lines, walls, jumps over liquid and holes, nearest cell when unreachable, allowed cells, limit, determinism, straightening; on the world map: round water, clicks on water, the reach, starting in water. In `pathfinding/`: `distance_map.rs`, `path.rs`, `find_path.rs`, `world_path.rs`, `straighten.rs`, `measure.rs`, `sized_path.rs` (`SizedPath`, `find_sized_path`), `straighten_spots.rs`; `sized_tests.rs` (open floor in one line, doors by size, jumping a stream, determinism), `slope_tests.rs` (round a steep hill, a good swimmer straight across a pool).

## Public API

- `DistanceMap::to(level, goal)` (Medium) and `DistanceMap::sized(level, size, goal)`: how long it takes to reach a goal from everywhere, for mobs of one size sharing the work, by the mobs' rule for that size (`movement::mob_step_sized`: never through a World Gate; bigger than Medium only where its circle fits between cells' middles, slower by the squeeze, so a Giant never gets through a doorway); `cost`, `towards`, `away` (steps by the same rule).

- `Path { steps, cells, cost, reached }`: the `Step`s to take, where each lands (not including the start), the time it takes in `movement::step_cost` units, and whether it ends on the goal.
- `find_path(level, from, to, max_cost, allowed) -> Path`: the quickest route, landing only on cells `allowed` accepts. If the goal can't be reached (or is past `max_cost`), goes to the reachable cell nearest it in a straight line: check `reached`.
- `find_path_by(level, from, to, max_cost, allowed, cost_of, gait)` and `straighten_by(..., cost_of, gait)`: the same for someone with this `movement::Gait` (steps by `step_on_level_by`, so a good swimmer swims water), each step priced by `cost_of(from, to)`, never less than `Gait::cheapest_per_mille` of `step_cost` (the estimate is scaled by it, so it stays a lower bound: a good swimmer or jumper goes quicker than they walk), such as the wilderness's slopes (`movement::wild_step_cost`); `Path::cost` is then in those units. `find_path` and `straighten` are them with `step_cost`.
- `straighten(level, from, &path, allowed) -> Path`: the same route, as quick and as legal, with its diagonals spread along each stretch instead of bunched. Separate because only looks care: skip it for anything that recalculates often.
- `find_sized_path(&LevelWay, &RoomMap, from: Spot, to: Spot, max_cost, allowed) -> SizedPath { spots, cost, reached }` (free positions, PR 2; the client's journeys on levels since PR 4; slopes and swimmers since PR 10): routes for a creature of the room map's size (`clearance::RoomMap`, `new_by` for a good swimmer) between any two spots on the way's level: A* over cells' middles with room, one step or a jump of two cells, each move checked by `clearance::check_segment_by` (a swimmer swims water rather than jumping it) and priced as moves are, `movement::LevelWay::cost` (slopes on its relief, wading, jumps by its gait; terrain left out) times its squeeze, in units of flat walking; the estimate is the straight-line distance scaled by `Gait::cheapest_per_mille`, less a unit a piece for rounding, so it stays a lower bound. Then `straighten_spots(&LevelWay, &RoomMap, spots)`: from each spot straight on to the furthest later one a single walk reaches for no more than going through those between (jumps kept), so routes go at any angle but never over a hill they went round. `spots` starts at `from` and ends at `to` if `reached`, else at the middle of the reachable cell nearest the goal.
- `find_world_path(ground, conditions, roads, rivers, from, to, gait) -> Path`: the same on the world map (`movement::WorldGround`, `WorldConditions`, `road::RoadSteps`), with slopes costing extra (less with `gait`'s Climbing: a climber goes nearer summits, tested), river points (`river::RiverPoints`) waded off roads, terrain slowing the rest (`movement::Footing`, `WorldGround::land_terrain` sampled once per `TERRAIN_STEP` square, only where a step would land off roads and water; the estimate counts on the cheapest terrain, `Gait::cheapest_terrain_per_mille`, as every land point has one), the sea swum (and entered near the coast by a good swimmer, `WorldGround::swimmable`, sampled once per point), its estimate scaled by the cheapest of the fastest road and the swimmer's wading and steps along roads less, searching only within `WORLD_ROUTE_REACH` (320 points each way, more than the view shows). Otherwise goes to the nearest point it can reach: check `reached`.

## Notes

- Moves come from `step_on_level` and costs from `step_cost`, so paths follow the movement rules by construction (corner cutting, jumps) and new rules arrive by themselves. Costs are time: straight 70, diagonal 99, a jump its distance.
- Deterministic: integers only, fixed direction order, ties settled by position, no hash maps.
- The estimate (`estimate`) must never exceed the real cost. Slower terrain is fine; terrain faster than floor has to be assumed by the estimate, as roads are on the world map.
- Asking for an unreachable goal searches everything reachable (within `max_cost`), so set a limit for monsters and spells. For many monsters chasing one goal, a distance map flooded from the goal (not built) would be cheaper than one search each; with terrain costs it must count the cost of walking towards the goal.
- World map search time (test build): 30-50 ms for a long click across hills (the slope cost makes the estimate a weaker guide); about 150 ms when the goal can't be reached (a click on water), which searches the whole area. Each point is sampled once per search, sampling being the slow part. `find_world_path` repeats `step_on_world_map`'s rule to use those samples: change them together. World routes aren't straightened: slopes make equally quick lines rare.
- `straighten` tries a line to the furthest point of each stretch and accepts it only if every step is legal and allowed and the cost is exactly the same. Jumps stay where they are.
- With any roads known, the world search's estimate assumes the fastest road (`road::FASTEST_PERCENT`, half the cost), so it stays a lower bound and routes are the quickest; that makes it a weaker guide. Measured (test profile, a 359-point road on the test world): 27 ms without roads, 58 ms with. With terrain (2026-11-08, the 303-point test route, alone): about 90 ms before terrain, 150 to 220 ms after (the search spreads further over uneven costs; sampling terrain more coarsely barely helped). With no roads it is unchanged. Tests in `road_tests.rs`: routes take a road where it is quicker, and say what they cost.
