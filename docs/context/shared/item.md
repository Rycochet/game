# shared: item

Ids, kinds of item, and carrying items in grids: the rules both sides check moves by. The server is the only place ids and new items come from.

## Files

- `crates/shared/src/id.rs`: `Id`, a version 6 UUID (text in saves, 16 bytes in messages). Nothing here makes a real one: `server::ids::new_id` does. `Id::stand_in(n)` (a version 8 UUID, never a server's) and `stand_ins()` name what a guess or a trial makes until the server names it.
- `crates/shared/src/item.rs`: the module list, the re-exports below (every type is used as `item::X`) and the constants `MAX_SHAPE_SIDE`, `MAX_DEPTH`, `INVENTORY_SIZE`. Tests for every rule below in `item/tests.rs`, `item/add_tests.rs`, `item/stack_tests.rs` and `item/look_up_tests.rs`. In `item/`:
  - `kind.rs`: `ItemKind` (`ALL`, `blade_family`, `comes_in_sizes` and other questions about a kind). `def.rs`: `ItemKind::def`, the table of what each kind is (the neck pieces' rows in `neck_def.rs`, footwear's in `feet_def.rs`, underclothes' with the old T-shirt in `under_def.rs`, with `ALL_UNDER`). `category.rs`: `ItemCategory`, `ItemKind::category`. `item_def.rs`: `ItemDef`, `ContainerDef`, `WearDef`, and the helpers the table is written with (`plain`, `bag`, `wear`, `taking`, `worn`).
  - `item.rs`: `Item` (`new`, `name`, `shape`, `total_weight`, `own_weight`, `set_gems`, `padded`, `bare`, `worn_locations`), `MadeAt`. `saved.rs`: how an `Item` is read, folding an old save's stacked coins into a count. `made.rs`: `Made`. `quality.rs`: `Quality`, `artifact_name`. `secrets.rs`: `Favour`, `Known`, and `Item`'s magic and secrets (`can_be_magical`, `roll_secrets`, `hide_secrets`).
  - `coin.rs`: `COINS`, `COINS_PER_NEXT`, `ItemKind::{coin_value, next_coin}`, `coin_stacks`, `coin_items`. `inventory/coins.rs`: coins turning into the next kind as they join a stack (`coin_room`, `carry_coins`), which `stack_room`, `stack_onto` and `add_to_stack` go through. Tests in `item/coin_tests.rs`.
  - `stack.rs`: `StackLimits`, and `Item`'s stacks (`count`, `is_counted`, `top`, `members`, `groups`, `looks_like`, `is_even`, `take_top`, `split_top`, `put_on`); `StackLimits::most_of(item)`: the most of it one stack could hold (1 for bags).
  - `look_up.rs`: `Item::find_within(id)` (itself, anything inside at any depth, or stacked on it), `Item::alone()` (a copy with nothing in it or stacked on it), `Inventory::find_within(id)` (the same through carried, worn and the bin: unlike `find`, it looks into stacks). For items looked up by id (`item_text`).
  - `placed.rs`: `Placed`, `Holder`. `refused.rs`: `MoveRefused`. `shape.rs`: `Shape`, `Rotation`. `drop.rs`: `Drop`, `ODDS_OUT_OF`, `roll_drop`.
  - `inventory.rs`: `Inventory`, its weights. `inventory/`: `lookup.rs` (`find`, `grid`, `placed`, what holds what), `rules.rs` (`check_move`, `check_add`, the rules below), `changes.rs` (`add`, `move_item`, the bin), `room.rs` (`first_place_for`, `give`), `stacking.rs` (`stack_room`, `stack_onto`, `add_to_stack`, `first_stack_for`, taking a stack's top), `parts.rs` (`part_of`, `lift_part`, `lift_count`, `lift_group`, `settle_parts`), `cost.rs` (`move_cost`, `put_down_cost`), `sort.rs` (`sorted_grid`, `sort_grid`).
- `crates/shared/src/item_form.rs`: `ItemForm` and the module list, re-exporting the types below. Tests. In `item_form/`:
  - `give.rs`: `give_form`, `give_form_from`, `give_blade_within`, `give_commission`. `give_to.rs`: `give_form_to`, which they all come down to. `make.rs`: `ItemOrder`, `make_item`, `try_make_item`, `batch_seed`. `armour.rs`: `Piece`, what each armour or footwear kind rolls. `limits.rs`: `Limits`.
  - Requests ("must be" options): `request.rs` (`RollRequest`, `Field`, `Refused`, `fields`, `keep_only`), `choices.rs` (`Choices`, `choices`), `ring_ask.rs` (a ring's options), `armour_ask.rs` (armour and footwear rolled again to a request), `armour_parts.rs` (reaching any piece's style, construction, material, finish and dyes), `armour_choices.rs` (armour's choices, by trying each value). Tests in `request_tests.rs`.
  - `gem.rs`: `GemForm`, `Cut`, rolling a gem. `ring.rs`: `RingForm`, `Setting` and the band's parts. `ring_roll.rs`: rolling a ring and its gems. `words.rs`: words for a ring's name (its gems counted, plurals).
  - `measure.rs`: `ItemForm::shape` and `grams_at`, for forms that decide their item's cells and weight.
- `crates/shared/src/material.rs`: `Material`, `Category`; tiers and densities in `material/properties.rs`. Placeholder table, first guesses at tiers. Tests.

## Public API

- `Item::bare()`: the kind's locations its form leaves bare (a sleeveless shirt's arms, footless hose's feet); `Item::worn_locations()`: where it is worn, the kind's less those. The wear rules and the clothes panel go by it.
- `Favour::{Blessed, Cursed}` (`Item.favour`, one or neither), `Known { magic, favour }` (`Item.known`, what has been found out). `Item::{can_be_magical, is_magical, roll_secrets, hide_secrets}`, `Inventory::hide_secrets`: see Notes. The rules for what they do are in `wear.rs` (`creature.md`).
- `Id(Uuid)`: something's id for good, for items, mobs, characters and accounts (the last two wrapped in `CharacterId` and `AccountId`). Prints as the usual UUID text, which also names files. `Id::parse` reads that text back (hyphenated only). `Id::for_test` is for tests in any crate. `Id::NOBODY` is nil, no real id.
- `ItemCategory::{ALL, name}` (Weapons, Armour, Clothing, Jewellery, Containers, Food, Valuables, Materials, Other: the owner's list), `ItemKind::category`: every kind in one, listed by hand with no catch-all, so a new kind needs one to build. For filtering and sorting grids.
- `ItemKind::{ALL, def()}`, `ItemDef { name, weight (grams), shape, container, wear, has_quality, counted, make_percent }` (`counted`: see Stacks), `WearDef` (see `creature.md`; `HELD` is every blade's: one hand, a size either way), `ContainerDef { width, height, grows, max_total, max_each }` (`grows`: a bag far bigger inside than out, such as the placeholder Bag of Holding, 3 × 3 outside (what goes in must still be smaller), 8 wide, up to 60 rows, 100 kg: its rules are those of the whole grid, and only clients draw just the rows in use; its weight is what really limits it). To add a kind: add it to the enum, `ALL` (alphabetical by name) and `def`. Saved by name.
- `Shape`: up to `MAX_SHAPE_SIDE` (8) a side, as bits of a `u64`, gaps allowed. `rectangle`, `has`, `cells`, `size`, `turned`, `scaled` (for a `Size`), `smaller_than`.
- `Rotation::{None, Quarter, Half, ThreeQuarters}`: clockwise; `next`, `turn_cell`.
- `Quality::{ALL, odds, roll, magic_reach}` (Poor to Artifact, odds out of `ODDS_OUT_OF`), `Drop { quality, kind, weight }`, `roll_drop` (both rolls: quality, then the item at it; a table without that quality takes the nearest below, or else above).
- `Item { id, kind, contents, made, quality, made_at, name, set, above, count }` (`count`: how many a counted stack is, 1 for anything else; `count()` counts a whole stack of either sort) (`name()` is its own name or its kind's with its size unless `Medium`; every artifact has its own, from `artifact_name`, placeholders so far; `shape()` is its kind's at the size it was made: use it, not `def().shape`), `MadeAt { world, minute }` (each world has its own clock), `Made` (who it was made for: `for_body`), `Placed { item, x, y, rotation }` (`shape`, `cells`), `Holder::{Inventory, Inside(id)}`.
- `Inventory { creature, outfitted, items, size, worn, bin }`: `find`, `placed`, `grid`, `grid_size`, `total_weight`, `weight_with_creature`, `check_move`, `move_item`, `check_add`, `add`, `first_place_for_new` (the same for something not carried), `first_place_for` (a closed bag: the first place, turning if it must), `give` (a new item, first place in the inventory; it, `stack_onto`, `add_to_stack` and `change_body` take a `new_id` for coins turning), `to_bin`, `to_bin_new`, `in_bin` (`bin` keeps what was last thrown away, and the next destroys it), `move_cost`, `put_down_cost` (what moves take, both sides), `sorted_grid`, `sort_grid` (a grid laid out again in one go: what looks the same stacked within the limits, then largest first by cells, then box, category, kind name and id, each in `first_room` from the top left keeping its turn unless it must turn; nothing changes if something wouldn't fit. Never ordered by an item's own name, which may hold secrets on the server, so both sides agree; it takes no time).
- `make_item(order, wearer, id, seed, made_at, new_id)`: a new item as an `ItemOrder { kind, size, magical, quality, set, favour, request }` asks (`ItemOrder::plain(kind, quality)`), made for the wearer's size and fingers, with its artifact name and form rolled from `seed`: the server's `made_for` passes the item's own id's seed, and Give Item a seed of its own so its preview on the client is what the server makes. Magic on something worn raises it to Uncommon; `set` only with quality, `favour` only for what is worn. `batch_seed(seed, n)`: the `n`th of a batch of different ones (the first is `seed` itself). `give_form_from(item, size, seed, new_id)`: `give_form` from a given seed. `try_make_item`: the same, `Err(Refused)` if its request can't be met; `make_item` then rolls it as usual.
- `RollRequest { style, construction, material, finish, dye, gem_material, gem_cut, settings, limits }` (all `Option`; `NONE`, `asked`, `without(field)`): what a new item's form must be, the rest rolled as usual. `material` is armour's, footwear's upper, underclothes', a ring's metal or a blade's blade metal; `settings` a ring's count; `limits` a blade's `Limits`. `give_form_to(item, size, seed, &request, new_id) -> Result<(), Refused>`: the entry point; with `NONE` it rolls exactly what `give_form_from` does. `Refused(Field)` names the option: one the kind doesn't take (`fields(kind)`), a value it can't have, or nothing rolled that keeps to it; the item is left alone. `choices(kind, quality, &request) -> Choices { styles, constructions, materials, finishes, dyes, gem_materials, gem_cuts, settings, limits }`: every value each option can take given the others asked for (its own is ignored), in its type's order, empty where the kind doesn't take it, so a caller never lists them itself.
- `give_form(item, size, new_id)`: rolls a new item's form, for kinds with one (gems, rings), going by its quality and its id's seed, and sets its size to what it is told or what it rolled. Everything making items calls it. `Item.form` holds the result, `None` for other kinds and old saves. `Item::set_gems`. `ItemForm::{name, gems}` (`Hands(HandsForm)` for gauntlets, demi-gauntlets and padded gloves; `Shoulders(ShouldersForm)` for shoulder armour, whose form also gives its cells and weight; `Torso(TorsoForm)` for torso armour and arming vests, likewise, and whether it is lined; `Head(HeadForm)` for arming caps, coifs, helmets and bevored helms, likewise; `Arms(ArmsForm)` for arm armour, likewise, and whether it is padded; `Legs(LegsForm)` for leg armour, chausses and padded chausses, likewise; `Waist(WaistForm)` for waist armour and cuirassier tassets, likewise; `Feet(FeetForm)` for foot armour, likewise; `Neck(NeckForm)` for collars, gorgets, bevors, aventails and mantles, likewise: see `armour.md`; `Footwear(FootwearForm)` for shoes, boots, sandals, clogs and slippers, likewise: see `footwear.md`; `Under(UnderForm)` for underclothes (shirts, braies, breast bands, loincloths, hose, socks, petticoats, linen coifs, wimples), likewise: see `underclothes.md`; `Set(Box<SetForm>)` for armour coats, corslets, half and full armour, whose weight is their pieces' and whose cells are their kind's), `GemForm { material, cut, shape_seed }`, `Cut::{ALL, name}`, `RingForm { metal, profile, width, pattern, settings }`, `Setting { angle, size, mount, gem }` (`takes`), `Profile`, `BandWidth`, `Pattern`, `Mount`.
- `Limits { fits, at_least_long, most_grams, least_grams, wielder, metal }`, `Limits::{NONE, allows}`: what a request needs of an item, so far only used by blades (`give_blade_within`, `give_commission`, a `RollRequest`'s `limits`, which may change an item's kind). `ItemKind::blade_family`: `Axe`, `Dagger`, `Knife`, `Longsword`, `Polearm`, `ShortSword`, `Spear`, `Sword` are rolled as blades (see `blade.md`).
- What an item gives while worn is `bonus::item_bonuses(item)` (`bonus.md`): armour only so far, by its own weight, quality and material.
- `Material::{ALL, GEMS, name, category, tier, density}`, `Category::{Bone, Gem, Leather, Metal, Plant, Textile, Wood}`. Armour's materials (owner's decisions): Steel, Bronze, Brass, Mithril, Adamantine (Metal); Horn, Dragon Scale, Chitin (Bone); Leather, Hardened Leather, Buff Leather (Leather); Linen, Wool, Cotton, Silk (Textile); Rattan, Cork, Coir (Plant). Footwear's: Suede, Rawhide, Fur (Leather), Felt (Textile), Straw (Plant). Underclothes': Hemp, Ramie, Haircloth (Textile). Rings roll only copper, gold, iron and silver, as before. Gems, cheapest first: Amethyst, Citrine, Garnet, Onyx, Peridot (tier 1), Aquamarine, Topaz (2), Emerald, Ruby, Sapphire (3), Diamond (4).
- Coins (owner, 2026-11-12): `ItemKind::{CopperCoin, SilverCoin, GoldCoin}` (1 g, 3 g, 5 g, Valuables, no quality; a saved `Coin` loads as copper), `COINS` (cheapest first), `COINS_PER_NEXT` (100), `ItemKind::coin_value` (in copper) and `next_coin`. `coin_stacks(copper, most)`: the fewest coins worth that, as (kind, count) stacks of at most `most`, gold first; `coin_items(copper, most, made_at, new_id)`: the same as items, one counted item each, for the server (mob money, quest rewards).
- Quest items (owner, quests plan; `item/quest_items.rs`): `Item::quest` names the quest one belongs to (each character's own). `is_quest_item`, `holds_quest_item` (itself, inside or stacked on), `strip_quest_items` (out of a bag at any depth), `Inventory::remove_quest_items(quest)` and `remove_quest_kind(quest, kind)` (carried, in bags, worn, the bin), `quest_items(quest)` and `quest_kind(quest, kind)` (how many carried). The server refuses giving one (so no shop takes one either), destroys one dropped (the client asks first) and takes a quest's away when it ends or is handed in.
- `ItemKind::Keepsake` (200 g, 1 × 1, Other, no quality): a quest's found item when it isn't a ring, necklace or weapon (a letter, book, toy...), named for what it is; one picture for all for now (owner).
- `ItemKind::Remains` (50 g, 1 × 1, Other, no quality, not counted: each is named for its creature and part, "Kobold Scales", which a counted stack couldn't tell apart): a "Collect" step's drop. Kinds only quests make are in `item/quest_def.rs`.
- `ItemKind::Parcel` (1 kg, 2 × 2, Other, no quality): a delivery's parcel, handed over on taking the quest, named for its town ("Parcel for Ashford").
- `ItemKind::Key` (30 g, Other, no quality) with `ItemForm::Key(lock id)`: opens that door lock (`door::Lock`); made by the server when it places locks, and by Key for This Door.
- `INVENTORY_SIZE` (6 × 4, for saves from before creature kinds and new inventories), `MAX_DEPTH` (3).

## Notes

- The inventory is the character's own grid, sized by their kind of creature (owner, 2026-11-04: it was called the pockets). (The item wipe of 2026-11-06 removed the aliases that read old saves.)
- Everything carried is one tree starting from the inventory and what is worn: a worn bag's grid works as any other. `placed` is `None` for something worn. An item's id goes with it through any move.
- What may go in a container, in the order refused: not into itself; only a shape with fewer cells that fits within the container's **outside** (so a 2 × 2 pouch holding a 4 × 4 grid takes rings but not a stone, and each step in is smaller, which alone stops endless nesting); no more than `MAX_DEPTH` grids deep, counting grids inside what is moved; `max_each` and `max_total` for every container it would end up in, counting contents at every level; then room in the grid.
- A container it is already in gains no weight from a move within it. Weights are checked outward: a bag inside another counts with all it holds against the outer one's `max_each`.
- Nothing in the table is small enough to go inside a `SmallBox` and have a grid, so `MAX_DEPTH` is only reached by bags made by hand in tests, for now.
- Rolls are the server's: it is the only maker of items. A roll's odds and colours are first guesses; colours are the client's (`inventory`). Quality gives nothing yet but armour's Toughness (`shared::bonus`). An item's quality may change, and its id doesn't. Design: `decisions.md`, 2026-11-02, item quality.
- Only what is worn, gems and blades come in sizes (`ItemKind::comes_in_sizes`); everything else is made `Medium`, whoever carries it or asks. Size changes only the outside of an item: each side is multiplied by `Size::quarters` (0.25 to 3, from the creature research's size bands), rounding down when enlarging and up when reducing, to at most 8. A blade's weight and cells come from its form, and its weight scales with its size cubed. A bag's grid, its limits and every other weight stay as they are, so a Tiny pouch is 1 × 1 and can hold nothing, and bags aren't rolled at other sizes yet. Saved items may now overlap: harmless, as they can still be moved apart, until the item wipe.
- A form is rolled once by the server and saved, never worked out again, as it will affect value: changing the rolls leaves existing items alone. The client only draws what it is sent. Each kind rolls its choices in order, each seeing those before: changing weights to lean on one (`Rng::pick_weighted`), or a weight of 0 to rule something out. Gems: quality, material, cut, size. Rings: quality, metal, band, number of settings, then each gem: first guesses for all of it. The finest gems never come Giant, by whole-number division.
- Requests keep rolls deterministic and leave the plain ones alone. Gems, a ring's metal and settings and a blade's metal are forced by giving every other option weight 0 (`keep_only`), which draws the same numbers, so nothing after them shifts; a value of weight 0 is refused (gold blades and five settings only with finer work, never uncut in a ring, no ring metal but copper, gold, iron and silver). Asking for a ring's gem means at least one setting, and every stone in it is as asked. Armour and footwear are rolled again (up to `TRIES`, 400) until style and construction match, with a style that makes the construction when none is asked (`Style::roll_allowing`), then given the material, finish and dye where the piece's `allowed()` grid takes them, as a set's pieces take their torso's; a finish must also suit the quality. A set's torso is rolled so and the rest around it (`set::roll_around`); a dye goes on every piece's cloth, cover and lacing. Hands and shoulders take no finish or dye (their cloth is a material, shoulders' finish leather's own). Armour's `choices` lists a value only if a roll asked for it is made from seed 0 or 1.
- A gem set in a ring is a whole item with its own id, inside the ring's form, so taking one out later needs only moving it, and the setting stays. Rolled: about 5% of settings empty, 90% of gems after the first the same as it, 3% one size too big for the setting (owner's request, though they find it ugly), no uncut stones. A ring's quality is how it was made, and a gem has its own. `Inventory::find` doesn't look in settings, as nothing can move them yet: taking them out and putting them in is to come, by `Setting::takes`. Nothing but `holds_or_is` and weights needs to see them so far.
- Secrets: only what is worn can be magical (better than common) or blessed or cursed (any quality), rolled once by `roll_secrets` for shop stock and mob gear, 5% each as first guesses. They stay secret from clients until found out: `hide_secrets` is what the server sends through, so a client sees `magical: false` and no favour. Known is per item, for everyone from then on, not per character. `Item::name` says them ("Cursed Magical Hat", "The Band (Cursed, Magical)"), going by what its copy has, so it is for clients: the server's copy would give them away.
- Kinds are placeholders for trying this out, and the weights are guesses.
- Stacks (owner's design, 2026-11-03): items that look the same as the player knows them (`looks_like`: the `hide_secrets` copies, ignoring ids, `made_at`, the `Known` flags (what is known shows already) and what is stacked on them) share one place. The first in is the bottom, whose id is the stack's and stays while things are added and taken off the top; the rest are in `Item::above`, the top last (LIFO), each with nothing above it. The top is what shows, is named, is worn (`wear` takes only it) and comes off first; everything else (moving, dropping, giving, the bin) takes the whole stack. Comparing what is known means a refusal gives no secret away, and a stack with something known to differ (`is_even` false) takes no more. Bags never stack. Counted kinds (`ItemDef::counted`, coins only so far: owner, 2026-11-12) are a number instead: one item with a `count` and nothing in `above`, its id and `made_at` the bottom's, whatever joins it; `members` is just it, and `room`, `most_of`, weights, `take_top`, `split_top` and `put_on` go by number. They have no quality, form, wear, grid, name or secrets (a test checks). Part of one is new, and so needs an id, which only the server makes: `split_top`, `take_top` and `lift_count` take `new_id` (the server's `ids::new_id`, or stand-ins for the client's guess, which the server's `Inventory` replaces), and `part_of` has no id to give for part of one. Where a part only joins another stack at once, its id is never kept (`Id::NOBODY`). `Inventory::take_top` (for wearing) gives nothing from a counted stack. Saves from before fold stacked coins into a count on load. Limits (`StackLimits`, the server's `HOW_STACK_GRAMS_PER_CELL`, 2000 g per cell of the shape, and `HOW_STACK_MAX`, 99, sent in `Welcome`; `NONE` stacks nothing): one item alone is always allowed. A bag's `max_total` counts the whole stack and its `max_each` one item of it. `give` stacks on the first stack it all fits on before finding room; dropping on a stack joins as much as fits, the rest staying where it was. Splitting: an id from inside a stack stands for it and everything above it (`part_of(stack, count)` names the top `count`); `lift_count(stack, count, new_id)` lifts that part of any stack, making a new one for a counted stack. `lift_part` takes that part out on its own, lying exactly where its stack does, so any move (`move_item`, `stack_onto`, `wear`, the bin, handing over) takes it as it would anything; `settle_parts` puts back on its stack any part still lying there (the only way two things share a place: an item of the same kind exactly where another is), as when a move was refused. Mixed stacks: once something in a stack is known to differ (`is_even` false), `groups` sorts it by look, and `lift_group(stack, items)` lifts some of it out together, both parts keeping their order, the same way; one going back goes on top.
- Coins turning (owner, 2026-11-12): whenever copper or silver joins a stack (`stack_onto`, `add_to_stack`, so `give` too) and it would reach 100, each 100 become one coin of the next kind, added to the stacks of that kind in the same grid with room, in order, or else as a new stack in its first free place (which may be where the joining coins or the whole old stack lay), carrying on upward; what is left keeps the stack's id unless none is left. All sums on counts. `stack_room` counts this: it tries each count from the most down on a copy of the inventory (with stand-in ids) until every new coin finds room, so what can't go stays where it came from. Gold stops at the usual limit. A new stack of coins has an id from `new_id`: the server's, or a stand-in in the client's guess until the server's answer (owner, see `decisions.md`). Coins short of 100 follow the usual limits (`HOW_STACK_MAX`), so a lower limit can keep them from ever turning.
