# Armour

Armour is worn piece by piece, one body place at a time, or as a [set](sets) that covers several places at once. Every piece is made in a style, built in one of a dozen ways, and made of a material, and all three show in how it looks. Armoursmiths in towns sell every kind; tailors sell the padding worn underneath.

These pages cover armour for people and other creatures that stand on two legs. Horse barding and caparisons exist but aren't described here yet.

## Where it goes

A body has places for clothes and armour: head, neck, shoulders, arms, torso, waist, hands, legs and feet, plus a back for a pack and a cloak. Each place takes things in layers, from the skin out:

| Layer | What goes there |
| ----- | --------------- |
| Skin | Shirts, socks, rings, an arming cap |
| Middle | Trousers, shoes and boots, padding: gambesons, arming vests, padded collars, coifs |
| Outer | Armour, and over the feet, sabatons |
| Over | Cloth worn over armour: surcoats and tabards |

Only one thing goes on each layer of a place. Something can only be put on or taken off when nothing on a higher layer covers it, so armour comes off before the padding under it. Some armour has its own padding and takes the Middle layer as well: you can't wear a gambeson under a full harness.

A piece counts only for the places it really covers: a mail coat that reaches the knee guards the waist, not the legs.

| Neck | [Neck armour](neck): gorgets, bevors, aventails, collars |

## How it is built

| Construction | What it is |
| ------------ | ---------- |
| Padded | Quilted layers of cloth |
| Leather | Hardened or thick leather |
| Mail | Interlinked rings |
| Ring mail | Rings sewn flat on leather |
| Scale | Small plates on a backing, overlapping downwards |
| Lamellar | Small plates laced to each other |
| Laminar | Strips riveted across leathers |
| Plates in cloth | Plates riveted inside or under cloth: brigandines |
| Mail and plate | Plates joined by mail |
| Splint | Long strips over a backing |
| Plate | Shaped solid plates, joined to move |
| Mirror | Large polished plates over mail |
| Woven | Rattan woven over a frame |

Not every place is made every way: there are no mirror gauntlets.

## Styles

Every piece has a style, and every piece of a set shares one. A style decides which ways of building a piece it uses and how the piece is shaped and finished.

| Style | Looks |
| ----- | ----- |
| White | Plain polished plates with rolled edges |
| Gothic | Pointed and ridged, flutes fanning out, cusped edges |
| Milanese | Smooth and rounded, broad plates |
| Maximilian | Parallel flutes over every plate |
| Greenwich | Etched bands with gilt lines |
| Kastenbrust | Boxy plates, long square-cut edges |
| Elven | Slender and leaf-pointed, no rivets showing |
| Dwarven | Thick, heavy plates with stepped edges |
| Japanese | Laced plates and lames, lacquer |
| Chinese | Lamellar, scale and studded cloth |
| Steppe | Mail and plate, lamellar, leather |
| Classical | Greek and Roman: bronze, linen, mail, strips |
| Byzantine | Lamellar and scale over mail |
| Indo-Persian | Mail and plate, mirrors, brass edging |

## Materials

| Kind | Materials |
| ---- | --------- |
| Metal | Iron, steel, bronze, brass, copper, and the rare mithril and adamantine |
| Leather | Leather, hardened leather, buff leather, rawhide |
| Textile | Linen, wool, cotton, silk |
| Bone | Bone, horn, and the rare dragon scale and chitin |
| Plant | Rattan, cork, coir |
| Wood | Wood |

Metal is finished bright, blackened, russet, blued, tinned, painted or lacquered; gilt and etched finishes are kept for rare pieces and better. Cloth covers and laces are dyed.

## Weights

The weights on these pages are for a piece made for an ordinary-sized person, in its usual material. Lighter materials such as mithril weigh less, heavier ones such as bronze more. A piece made for a bigger or smaller creature weighs more or less with the square of its size: twice the size, four times the weight.

Gem sockets in armour are planned.
